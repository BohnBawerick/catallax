# 3.0A gap rule for imports

Step 6 of roadmap item 3.0A: decision Q9 of the [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), and step 2 of the A+ checker in section 4.2.

> Own builds: same-parameter gap. Imports: geometric gap first; if the parametric gap is much larger, reparametrise instead of raising the tolerance.

> Imports whose copies run at different speeds: measure the geometric gap both ways, reparametrise, then continue with the same-parameter gap.

The code is `crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/import_gap.rs` (the rule), `gap/geometric.rs` (the geometric gap) and `gap/refit.rs` (the change of parameter). [The interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-21-import-gap-rule.md) lists what callers see. It builds on the [checker](3.0A-edge-gap-checker.md), [copy tagging](3.0A-copy-tagging.md) and the [tolerance rules](3.0A-tolerance-rules.md).

## Why imports need their own rule

The kernel reads both copies of an edge at one parameter, so the tolerance of an edge use has to cover its same-parameter gap. An own build makes both copies in one parameter, and that gap is what admission measures and stores. Nothing changes for own builds.

An import promises no shared parameter. ISO 10303-42 ties no parameter of a 3D curve to the parameter of its copy on a surface. Two copies can trace the same curve and still be far apart at equal parameters, because one runs ahead of the other. Storing that distance would raise a tolerance to cover a difference of parameter, with nothing wrong in the geometry.

Two of the fourteen known-answer edges are exactly this. Edge 8 is the stretch of solidcore #570, 5.7e-5 mm of same-parameter gap on one straight line. Edge 14 is the rational circle of solidcraft #8502, 0.0436 mm between a circular arc and a copy that traces the same arc at a constant angular rate.

## The rule

`numerical::admit_import` admits the definition as imported first, as before. Then, for every edge use:

1. A use is left alone when nothing could be lowered or when the two gaps are equal by construction (the list is below).
2. The same-parameter gap is the one admission just read with the A+ checker. The rule uses that reading and does not take it again.
3. The geometric gap is measured, both ways.
4. Where the same-parameter gap is much larger than the geometric gap, the face copy is given the parameter of its 3D curve. The new copy is kept where the checker reads its same-parameter gap much lower than the old one's.

If any copy changed, the definition is admitted again with the new copies. Admission stores rule T4's 1.1 times the same-parameter gap of each new copy, as the full checker read it when the change of parameter took it as its last candidate. That is "then continue with the same-parameter gap". The checker reads each copy once in the whole admission. The geometric gap is never stored.

`NumericalBody::import_gaps()` returns one row per use that was read geometrically: both gaps, the tolerance of the reparametrised copy where one was made, its spans, the work counted against the use's one allowance, and the outcome (`Geometric`, `Reparametrised`, `NotLowered` or `NotAdmitted`).

Rule T3 comes after. A stored tolerance still over the cap is refitted from the 3D curve, then the user may opt in, as the fifth part built it. Its refit and opt-in rules are unchanged. Inside an import admission its work is on the same accounts as the rule's (below).

### "Much larger"

The decisions give no ratio. The rule uses the one number rule T4 already has, its margin of 1.1, and adds no constant.

A same-parameter gap within 1.1 times the geometric gap stores no more than rule T4's tolerance of a gap that size already covers. Above that, the stored tolerance would be covering the parameter, which is what Q9 says not to do. The same test is used three times: to decide to reparametrise (same-parameter gap against geometric gap), to stop doubling spans (new same-parameter gap against geometric gap), and to keep the new copy (old tolerance against new).

### What is left alone

- A derived copy. Admission does not measure it, and imports have none.
- A curve projected on a plane. It has no u/v of its own, and its gap is the distance of the 3D curve from the plane, which is geometric already.
- A straight residual: a line against a face copy whose lift is also straight. Both run at constant speed, so their largest distance is at an end, and the geometric gap pairs the ends with each other too. The two gaps are equal.
- A use whose stored tolerance is at rule T1's least floor, 1e-7 mm. No tolerance is stored under the floor, so no change of parameter could lower it. This is what keeps a clean import at the cost it had.

## The geometric gap

`gap::measure_geometric`. The largest distance from a point of one copy to the other copy as a curve in space, read in both directions: 3D curve to lifted face copy, and lifted face copy to 3D curve.

1. The two ends are paired with each other, first with first and last with last. A copy that is slid along its curve shows here: its geometric gap is its same-parameter gap, and it is not reparametrised.
2. Between the ends, each point is paired with its foot on the other copy. The foot is found by Gauss-Newton with a forward-difference rate (Dennis and Schnabel 1983, sections 5.4 and 10.1), starting from the other copy's point at the same parameter, inside the edge's range. A step that does not come nearer is not taken. So no pair is further apart than the same-parameter pair it started from, and the geometric gap is never read larger than the same-parameter gap.
3. The points are those of the checker's own plan: the edge is cut at every break (step 3) and each piece gets `max(17, 3 x composed degree)` Chebyshev points (step 4). The count depends on the shape of the edge alone.
4. Every local maximum of the sampled foot distances is polished with Brent's method (step 6). The polish is one function now, shared with the same-parameter search.

The coefficient tail of step 5 is not read here. A foot distance has kinks where the foot crosses a knot of the other copy, slightly off the breaks of the plan, and a tail that never goes quiet would double points to the work cap. The reading only decides. A foot that settles on a wrong branch is still a point of the other copy, so it can only read a distance too large, and that keeps a copy as imported.

Both directions are needed. On the seeded smooth surface family one edge reads 3.60e-4 mm at the same parameter, 3.60e-4 mm from the face copy to the 3D curve, and 1.76e-4 mm from the 3D curve to the face copy: the lifted copy has a bump, and a point of the straight 3D curve finds the bump's flank nearer than its top. One direction alone would call that gap half speed.

The work is counted like the checker's: one unit per break and per halving while planning, one per evaluation of either copy. The reading draws on what admission's same-parameter reading left of the use's one allowance, as the ends reading of a projected copy does, and refuses with `numerical.gap-work-cap` where that runs out.

## The change of parameter

`gap::refit::reparametrise`. The new copy is the old copy read where its lift is nearest the 3D point of each parameter. It runs along the old copy in u/v, so the face's boundary does not move, and its two ends are the old ends.

It shares its fit with the refit of rules T3, T7 and T8. The nodes are the breaks of the 3D curve and the places where the old copy's knots land on it. Each span is the cubic Hermite piece of two node values and their rates (Farin, section 7.5), and the spans double until the checker reads the target or 256 spans are reached. The only difference from the refit is what a node reads: the refit takes the foot of the 3D point on the surface, and gets a new copy under the 3D curve; the change of parameter takes the old copy at the foot's parameter, and gets the old copy at a new speed.

The nearest points must ascend along the old copy as the 3D curve is run through. A copy that folds back has no change of parameter; it is kept as imported, its row says `NotLowered`, and its tolerance covers the same-parameter gap.

## Work and the per-edge cap

> A hard per-edge work cap refuses by name.

That is section 4.2 step 8. The cap is the checker's, 1,000,000 units for one edge, and its refusal is `numerical.gap-work-cap`. Neither number nor name changed. Since 3.0B the allowance belongs to the topological edge and its uses share it. Before that each use had one of its own. See [the one-model note](3.0B-one-model.md#the-work-cap-of-the-edge-gap-checker-is-one-allowance-for-an-edge).

An import admission has one allowance per edge for the whole admission, and a record of what was read of each edge use (`edge_gap::Accounts`). Each step draws on what the steps before it left, on this use and on the edge's other uses.

| Step | What is counted |
|---|---|
| Admission's reading of the copy as imported | The checker's units for the same-parameter gap. The reading is kept in the account with its work. |
| The geometric gap, both ways | One unit per break and per halving while planning, one per evaluation of either copy. |
| The change of parameter | One unit per evaluation of either copy while a foot is found, one per reading of the old copy, and the checker's units on every candidate, kept or not. Nothing starts again when the spans double. |
| The admission of the new copies | Nothing. It uses the readings in the accounts: admission's for every copy that did not change, and the checker's reading of the last candidate for a new copy. |
| Rule T3's refit of a use still over the cap | One unit per evaluation while a foot is found, and the checker's units on every candidate, kept or not. |
| Rule T3's probe at the opt-in limit and its admissions of the refitted body | Nothing. They use the readings in the accounts, and the checker's reading of the last candidate for a refitted copy. |

So admission's own reading of a use is inside the allowance, and no copy is read a second time under a new cap. The rule does not read the same-parameter gap again. No admission after the first reads at all, and no refit starts a new cap.

Where new copies are refused and rule T3 goes back to the copies as imported, each account goes back to the reading of its copy as imported. What was counted for the use stays counted.

Every unit is also charged to the operation, so the 20M target and the 100M allowance see the same work.

A use that spends its allowance at any step refuses the admission by name. Nothing read before that stands in for the answer: not the copy as imported, and not a candidate from before the allowance ran out. If the admission of the new copies comes back with `numerical.gap-work-cap`, with the operation's allowance spent or with the deadline passed, `admit_import` returns that refusal. The body as imported was admitted earlier in the same call, and it is still not returned in its place. The probe of rule T3's refit keeps the name of such a refusal too.

Own builds, continuations and exports do not call the rule. Since 3.0B each of their admissions has the same accounts, one allowance per edge, and the refits of rules T7 and T8 draw on the allowance of their use's edge and share accounts with the admission that follows them.

### Reparametrise or refit

They answer different faults, and the geometric gap tells them apart.

| Fault | Same-parameter against geometric | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| The copies trace the same curve at different speeds | much larger | Change of parameter (Q9). The tolerance falls to the geometric gap's. |
| The face copy is off the 3D curve, which lies on the surface | equal | Kept. Over the cap, rule T3's refit puts a new copy under the 3D curve. |
| The 3D curve is off the surface | equal | Kept. No copy can lower it; over the cap the user may opt in. |

## Which kernel this follows

OpenCASCADE, read from its source (7.8.1).

- `BRepLib_ValidateEdge::processApprox` measures an edge that is not flagged same-parameter by projection both ways: each of 22 even points of one curve is projected on the other with `Extrema_LocateExtPC`, seeded at the proportional parameter, and the two ends are compared directly. The geometric gap here is that reading on the checker's plan in place of 22 even points, with every local maximum polished.
- `Approx_SameParameter` pairs the copies by projection, keeps the two ends, asks the pairs to ascend, and builds a new face curve in the 3D curve's parameter. `BRepLib::SameParameter` keeps it when its deviation is not larger than the old one's. The change of parameter here pairs the same way. It differs in the fit: OpenCASCADE interpolates a cubic change-of-parameter function and approximates the composition with `AdvApprox`; here the composition is interpolated directly by Hermite spans, the fit the tolerance rules already use, and every candidate is measured by the A+ checker instead of twice 22 even steps times 1.05.

SMLib, read from its source (the public tree in `NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep`: `SmGap.cpp`, `SmEdgeuse.cpp`, `SmBrep.cpp`).

- SMLib ties no parameter of a u/v trim to its edge. `SmCrvCrvGapFunction::Evaluate` drops each of 20 sample points of the edge curve on the lifted trim curve, with a local solve from its neighbour's parameter and a global solve where that fails. `SmCrvSrfGapFunction` drops them on the surface. So every SMLib edge gap is a geometric gap, read one way. The reading here is the same kind, in both directions and on the checker's plan.
- `SmBrep::ValidateAndUpdateTolerances` rebuilds the trim where the gap to the trim is bad and the gap to the surface is good, "rather than increasing the edge tolerance". That is the case of rule T3's refit. It never meets a difference of speed, because it never pairs at one parameter.

Catallax does pair at one parameter, as OpenCASCADE does, so it follows OpenCASCADE for the difference of speed and agrees with SMLib that a tolerance covers geometry only. Neither kernel has a ratio for "much larger": OpenCASCADE acts when a sample is off at the same parameter by more than its working tolerance.

## Choices to confirm

- **"Much larger" is rule T4's 1.1.** The decisions state no ratio, and I added none. A larger ratio would accept tolerances raised for speed up to that ratio.
- **A use at the floor is not read.** The geometric gap is read where a tolerance could be lowered, not on every use. Reading it on every use changes no stored tolerance and costs 6 to 14 times the checker's evaluations per use.
- **The geometric gap is not in the evidence.** It is in the rows of `import_gaps()`, which are not stored with the body. The evidence holds the same-parameter record of the copy that was admitted.
- **A body that is not admitted with its new copies is returned as imported**, with `NotAdmitted` rows, when the refusal is a verdict on the new copies. A refusal that says work or time ran out is returned as it is: the per-edge work cap, the operation's allowance, the deadline.

## Limits

- Admission's reading and everything the rule does with a use share one work allowance, as section 4.2 step 8 has one hard cap per edge. Since 3.0B the edge's other uses draw on it too. A use that fits the same-parameter reading and not the rest refuses with `numerical.gap-work-cap`, where an own build of the same copies is admitted. On the 256-span known-answer edge the two readings alone take about 378,000 of the 1,000,000 units.
- The geometric gap is a sampled search, like the checker: a feature narrower than the spacing of its points, with no knot, is missed. It can only make the rule keep a copy it could have reparametrised, or try a change of parameter the checker then measures.
- A face copy must already be given in its edge's parameter range. A file's face curve with a range of its own needs the affine map of the range first (OpenCASCADE's `SameRange`). The numerical importers carry no file face curves into this admission. The native tolerant admission reads a file's face curves through the rule, one use at a time, where their interval holds the edge's, and keeps none the rule did not read ([check once](3.0A-check-once.md), "The native tolerant admission of a file that records nothing").

## Later

- The tolerant import (`catallax-import/src/tolerant/admission/fit.rs`) reads every edge use that carries a file face curve through this rule since the check-once step: `read_import_use` is the rule on one use, and the checker reads the file's own carriers through evaluators (`gap::SampledCurve`, `gap::SampledSurface`). Its 17 stations stay for what the admission builds itself: vertices, faces and the traces it projects. Moving those onto the checker belongs with the one-model work of 3.0B.
- A numerical import that keeps the file's own face curves as retained u/v curves would reach this rule with real differences of speed. Today's builders make straight u/v copies from the end points.
