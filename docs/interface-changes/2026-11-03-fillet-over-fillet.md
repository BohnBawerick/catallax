## 2026-11-03: a fillet rolls over earlier fillets

Pieces: features, field, step, app
Change: a later fillet now builds where it meets earlier elementary fillets in these cases, each of which refused before.

- Under roll-over (`catallax-recipe/4` and later), a selected edge's smooth chain continues over the end of an earlier cylinder strip as a torus strip about that strip's axis. Examples are a block's top edge after its uprights were rounded, a pocket's floor edge after its corners were rounded, a box edge reaching an earlier rounded edge, and an L's end edge reaching its inner round. Convex over convex, concave over concave and convex over concave strips all build. Listing every edge of such a chain explicitly in `catallax-recipe/3` builds the same body. These refused with `fillet.unsupported-overflow` ("a non-end neighbour intersects the blend carrier").
- Where the new radius equals the earlier strip's, the ball sits on that strip's axis and does not move, so the chain's piece over it is a sphere strip. It is a sector of the ball bounded by the earlier strip's cross-section and the two neighbouring strips' meridians. Its contact on the plane collapses to one point, where the two neighbouring strips' rails meet. That plane face loses the input edge there and keeps no zero-length edge. These refused with `fillet.unsupported-contact` ("this contact requires a horn or spindle torus"), and with `fillet.radius-too-large` at that limit since [the contact-limit change](2026-11-03-fillet-contact-limit-equality.md).
- An `"all"` selection now leaves out smooth edges, which no ball rounds. These are an earlier strip's contact rails, recognised from that strip's retained definition whatever its carrier, and analytic edges whose faces are tangent along their whole length. Rounding every edge of a body that already has fillets builds, including after an oblique plane-cylinder band, whose elliptic rails no analytic test would call smooth. It refused with `fillet.unsupported-topology` ("tangent faces do not define a regular sharp edge"). A smooth edge chosen by an explicit reference still refuses with that code.
- An oblique plane-cylinder band is bounded by the hull of its carrier's controls, which have positive weights, so another face or strip whose bounds miss that box is proved clear of it. A strip near such a band, and such a band near a curved face, now build where that box separates them. They refused with `fillet.unsupported-overflow` or an `intersect.*` code.
- Under roll-over, a strip may end where two earlier strips meet at a mitre, each tangent to one of the strip's faces. Rounding a box's uprights after its top loop, or a pocket's uprights after its floor loop, is an example. When the three strips form the trihedral corner they would have closed together, with equal radii and independent planar supports, the end closes with that corner's `corner.vertex` sphere sector. The sector's arcs trim both earlier strips and the mitre between them goes. The body is the one that rounding all those edges at once gives. These refused with `fillet.unsupported-overflow`. A mitre of strips of another radius now refuses with `fillet.unsupported-corner`. An `"all"` selection leaves out a mitre that such a corner swallows, since a ball of its radius has one centre there and rounds nothing more. It refused with `fillet.unsupported-contact`.
- `FilletDefinition::surface` can be a `Surface::Sphere`. On such a sphere strip, the plane contact in `contacts` is a `Circle` of zero radius at the point where the cylinder's axis meets the plane. New `FilletDefinition::point_contact` returns that contact's index, and new `FilletDefinition::arc_hull` bounds an arc strip's region over one arc of its edge. `Built::fillets` holds sphere strips and keeps one definition per strip, as before. Strips continued from one selected edge share its `blend.edge` origin.
- A closed ring whose ball is as wide as its cylinder, such as a boss's top rim rounded at the boss's radius, consumes the face inside the ring. Its definition now reduces to the sphere, and the build still refuses it with `fillet.radius-too-large` at the cylinder's radius, as the contact-limit change reports it. The field keeps its earlier refusal for that case. The field compiler is otherwise unchanged and still refuses the fillets above with `fillet.unsupported-overflow`.
- STEP export probes a trimmed periodic face with meridians leaning toward its reference direction, once the existing probes find no crossing. A trimmed torus whose arc lies on that side now writes charts. Before, such a face fell back to the legacy encoding, or refused with `step.unsupported-geometry` for a torus sheet. Scenes that the existing probes chart write the same bytes.

Why: the release needs fillets that meet earlier fillets on ordinary parts, such as rounded bosses and pockets, boxes rounded in several steps and every-edge rounds after a first round. Equal radii are the common case, and their exact result is the sphere strip or corner sector, not a horn torus.

Migration: SolidDraft sees bodies where it saw the refusals listed above, and needs no call changes. A ball wider than the earlier round it would roll over still refuses with `fillet.radius-too-large`. Its radius limit is that round's radius, so "Try a radius under x mm" remains true, and a chain at a radius equal to x now builds. Code that reads `Built::fillets` must accept a `Surface::Sphere` carrier and a zero-radius plane contact, and can find it with `point_contact`. Fillet-over-fillet cases not built yet refuse with typed codes:

- a strip ending on an earlier round without roll-over: `fillet.unsupported-overflow`;
- a mitre of another radius: `fillet.unsupported-corner`;
- a chain over an earlier round's elliptic end, where the round meets a leaning face: `fillet.unsupported-corner`;
- a smooth edge chosen by name: `fillet.unsupported-topology`.
