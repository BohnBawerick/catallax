# 3.0A check once

Step 5 of roadmap item 3.0A. [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.1 item 5:

> Check once. Export writes the numbers and adds the rounding error to the tolerance; it does not re-admit the body. Reopening trusts the tolerance recorded at export.

A numerical body is measured once, when it is admitted. Before this step it was measured three times: at admission, again at export on the rounded decimals, and again when the file was opened. [The interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-21-check-once.md) lists what callers see.

## What the other kernels do

Checked before writing, as the roadmap asks. Source: `docs/research/cp2-grill/t-smarter-research.md`, section 3, and the decisions, section 9.2.

- **SMLib** caches the largest gap of an edge after one expensive call (`GetMaxEdgeFaceGap`), and its exports write the numbers.
- **OpenCASCADE** checks in floating point, on demand, once. Its STEP writer writes the numbers and one uncertainty for the file (`write.precision.mode`). Its reader takes that uncertainty as its precision: `read.precision.mode` defaults to `File (0)`, "the precision value is set to length_measure in uncertainty_measure_with_unit from STEP file" (STEP translator user guide, OCCT 8.0.1; the reader's line was read this session, the writer's parameter is from a summary of the same page).
- **Parasolid** stores each edge and vertex tolerance in the XT file and takes them as they are on load.

This step follows all three: the export writes the numbers, and the reader takes the tolerance the file states. STEP has one uncertainty per file where XT has one per entity, so the file records one tolerance. None of the three adds the rounding of its own decimals to the tolerance it writes. The decisions ask for that term, and it is the one piece here with no kernel behind it. Its method is below.

## Export

`catallax_step::numerical::build` has three writers: lines on planes, analytic bodies, and bodies with spline carriers or retained u/v curves. Each rounded the body's tables to the written digits and then called numerical admission on the rounded tables: every edge end, every edge use, every loop and rule T5 again. That call is gone from all three. So are the 128-bit samples of each written u/v line on an analytic face.

What each writer still does is what a file needs to be readable: it refuses a direction that rounds to nothing, parameters that collapse, a circle whose written start does not select its zero phase, and a scalar over the caller's rounding limit. Those are in the writers as before.

### The rounding error

`crates/catallax-step/src/numerical/rounding.rs`. The rounding error of a file is a bound on how far the rounding of its numbers can move a point of a written curve or surface, to first order. The method is the forward error bound of a perturbed input (Higham, "Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms", 2nd edition, section 1.6): the error of each number times how far a unit change of it moves a point.

The writers already kept the largest rounding error of each kind of number. The bound multiplies each by a lever read from the values the file holds.

| Kind of number | Largest error | Bound on the movement | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordinate or radius | `e_L` | `(sqrt(3) + 2) e_L` | Three coordinates of a point; two radii of a torus. |
| Line magnitude | `e_M` | `T e_M` | A line's point at parameter `t` is its origin plus `t` times the magnitude. |
| Direction ratio, cone semi-angle | `e_D` | `24 M e_D` | A direction of three ratios turns by `sqrt(3) e_D`, a placement has two, and no point is farther than `4 sqrt(3) M` from a placement's origin. |
| Knot, trim parameter, u/v coordinate, u/v line coefficient | `e_P` | `(15 + 2T + (10 + 4T) U) V e_P` | Below. |
| Weight | `e_W` | `(4 sqrt(3) M + 4 V Q) e_W / w` | A weight moves a rational point by its share of the distance to a control, over the smallest weight. |

The levers: `M` is the largest coordinate or radius, `T` the largest edge parameter, `V` the largest speed of a curve or surface in millimetres per parameter unit, `U` the largest speed of a written face copy in u/v units per unit of the edge parameter, `Q` the largest u or v of a face copy, `w` the smallest weight.

The parameter term adds up these parts. A knot moves a spline's point by at most its speed over its degree, and a point depends on `2p + 2` knots (Piegl and Tiller, "Computing the derivative of NURBS with respect to a knot", Computer Aided Geometric Design 15, 1998), which is `4 V e_P` for a curve and `8 V e_P` for the two directions of a surface. A trim parameter moves an edge's end by `V e_P`. A face copy's u/v point moves by `(1 + T + (5 + 2T) U) e_P`, from its controls, knots and trim parameters, or from the origin, magnitude and direction of a u/v line. The surface carries that to space at `2 V`.

A spline's speed is bounded by its hodograph's controls, `p (P[i+1] - P[i]) / (u[i+p+1] - u[i+1])` (Piegl and Tiller, "The NURBS Book", equations 3.4 to 3.6), times the square of its largest weight over its smallest for a rational one. That factor is Floater's for a rational Bezier curve ("Derivatives of rational Bezier curves", Computer Aided Geometric Design 9, 1992), taken here for a rational spline as a lever, not as a proof.

The levers are read from the values the file holds, not from the body's own parameters, so two bodies that write the same file record the same tolerance.

This is a first-order bound and it is coarse on purpose: each term takes the largest error of its kind and the largest lever of the body. At 17 digits it is far under rule T1's floor on every body measured (5e-11 mm on the R2 rim, against a floor of 1e-7 mm). It matters when a caller asks for few digits.

### What the file records

`recorded = largest entity tolerance + rounding error`, rounded up to the written digits. The largest entity tolerance already holds rule T4's 1.1 from the body's admission. Nothing multiplies it again, and the rounding error is added, not scaled.

The stamp in `FILE_DESCRIPTION` gains two fields after the ones it had:

```
...;rounding_error_mm=5.0005829242261075e-11;recorded_tolerance_mm=1.0005000582924227e-7
```

`NumericalStepReport::uncertainty_mm` is that tolerance, and `rounding` holds the errors, the levers and the bound. With a receiver profile (rule T8) the file's global uncertainty is the same number. Rule T8 defined it as the largest entity tolerance of the source and of the check of the written values; there is no such check now, and the rounding error stands for it. Without a profile the header has no uncertainty entity, as before, so only the stamp changes.

### What an export no longer refuses

Admission on the rounded tables refused where rounding pushed a written value over the body's limit, or left two entities too close for rule T5, or broke a loop. None of that runs. The file is written, and its record says how far its values can be from the body's. Two controls show it:

- `rounded-gap` (`numerical_uv_join`): one significant digit opens a u/v join to 0.012 mm on a body held to 0.01 mm. It was refused. It is now written with a recorded tolerance of 0.2 mm, and the audit finds the written join inside that.
- The oblique tube at one digit, held to 1e-8 mm. It was refused. It is now written, with a record far over 1e-8 mm. Its audit refuses by rule T5: a full admission still finds the body too loose for its 1 mm features.

The caller's `max_scalar_rounding_error_mm` still refuses a length that rounds by more than it allows. That limit is 1e-7 mm by default, so an export at the default digits writes the same geometry it wrote before.

### The audit

`catallax_step::numerical::audit_written` writes the same bytes and then runs the admission the export used to run, on the written values, under a limit of 1.1 times the recorded tolerance. A stored record is 1.1 times a measured gap (rule T4), so a refusal means a gap measured on the written values is over what the file records. It is for tests and CI, as the audit of derived copies is (section 4.4). The tests that read the old emitted evidence call it, and so do the three regression controls that printed that evidence (`numerical_loft_cut`, `numerical_uv_join`, `numerical_import`).

## Reopening

### The record

`catallax_import::recorded::recorded_tolerance(bytes)` reads the header only. A file whose description starts with `catallax numerical approximate` and holds `recorded_tolerance_mm` records that value. A scaled export records it in the model's millimetres and is read at its scale.

A file written before this step has no such field. Its `source_tolerance_mm` is the limit its export admitted the written values under, so every gap of that file was measured at or under it, and it is taken as the record. The R2 rim file is such a file.

By the owner's ruling of 20 November 2026 nothing binds the record to the geometry under it: no hash, no fingerprint. A file edited by hand after export opens with the tolerance it states, as an XT file opens with the tolerances it stores. `nothing_binds_the_record_to_the_geometry_under_it` shows it.

### Where it is used

A STEP file opens through three admissions, and each now takes a recorded tolerance in place of a measurement.

- **Lines on planes** (`numerical::import_step`, planar path) and **analytic bodies** (`numerical/analytic.rs`). `catallax_topology::numerical::admit_recorded` admits the definition. Each edge end and each edge use holds the recorded tolerance under its own check name, `edge.vertex-recorded-tolerance` and `trim.edge-surface-recorded-tolerance`, so a recorded value is never read as a measurement. Rule T4's margin is not applied to it. Every vertex and edge of the body has it as its tolerance.
- **Native tolerant bodies** (`tolerant/admission`), the path the R2 rim takes. A new first pass reads each edge use's face copy as the file wrote it: the u/v curve on a curved face, the plane's own coordinates on a plane. Every vertex, edge and face takes the recorded tolerance. No point is projected on a surface and no trace is rebuilt. `TolerantSolid::recorded_tolerance_mm()` says the body was taken this way.

The record is used only where the reader takes the file's entities as they are. A planar source the reader repaired, a cone it fitted, a seam it derived or a candidate it healed is measured as before. A native body the first pass cannot read as written (a curved face's use with no u/v curve, a u/v curve outside its surface's charts, an edge that runs past its u/v curve by more than the recorded tolerance) is handed to the measured passes, with a `recorded-tolerance-unused` warning that says why. So a record never makes a file harder to open.

The body's limit after opening is the caller's or the recorded tolerance, whichever is larger. Rule T3's caps are for files that are measured.

### What is still done

- **The structure.** The topology, each carrier's definition and each loop's simple, oriented u/v walk are checked. They are what makes the tables a body. No curve is evaluated against a surface.
- **Edge ends.** A STEP edge has no parameter range. Where the file's curve is not trimmed, each end is still placed on its curve from its vertex, as any reader must. The distance found is not kept.
- **Rule T5** is not run again. It compares distances, and reopening measures none.
- **The exact attempts** are unchanged: the certified import is tried first, and an exact upgrade of a native body last, as for every import. On the R2 rim they are 0.38M and 3.36M of the 11.5M. They try a proof; they do not measure a gap.

### Reopening and the gap rule for imports

Decision Q9, [the gap rule for imports](3.0A-import-gap-rule.md), landed while this step was open: "Own builds: same-parameter gap. Imports: geometric gap first; if the parametric gap is much larger, reparametrise instead of raising the tolerance." The rule is in `catallax_topology::numerical::admit_import`. This is what each way of opening a STEP file does with it.

| Path | File records no tolerance | File records a tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Certified import, tried first | Exact. No gap is stored. | The same. |
| Numerical import: lines on planes, analytic bodies, sewn constructed surfaces (`catallax-import/src/numerical.rs`, `numerical/analytic.rs`, `numerical/analytic/constructed.rs`) | `admit_import`: every edge use goes through the gap rule. | `admit_recorded`: nothing is measured, so the rule has no gap to read. |
| Upgrade of a native body to a numerical one (`tolerant/opened.rs`) | `admit_import`, through the same admission. | `admit_recorded`. |
| Native tolerant admission, the R2 rim's path (`tolerant/admission/fit.rs`) | Its own reading, not the rule's code. | The recorded pass: nothing is measured. |

So two cases do not run the rule's code.

A file that records a tolerance is not measured at all. That is this step's decision, and it agrees with Q9: the file is an own build written out, its two copies of an edge were made in one parameter, and its record is the same-parameter tolerance of that build plus the rounding error. No tolerance is raised to cover a difference of speed, because none is raised.

The native tolerant admission of a file that records nothing does not call the rule. Its own reading drops 17 points of each edge on the surface, which is a geometric reading one way, and it rebuilds a file's face curve that disagrees at the same parameter instead of raising the tolerance. That is Q9's intent by another route, without the A+ checker and without the reading in both directions. It is the next step, and the gap rule's note names it too: its bodies are not the numerical model `admit_import` reads, and a file's face curve has a range of its own that needs mapping to its edge's first. Moving it belongs with the one-model work of 3.0B.

## Choices to confirm

- **One tolerance per file.** The file records the largest entity tolerance, so a reopened body has that on every entity. STEP has one uncertainty per file, and rule T8 writes the largest. The next operation measures each use again (rule T7) and the tolerances come back down.
- **An older file's `source_tolerance_mm` counts as its record.** Its export admitted the written values under it. Without this the R2 rim file itself would still be measured.
- **The exact attempts stay.** The certified import first and the exact upgrade last are 3.7M of the rim's 11.5M. Skipping them for a file that records a tolerance is a few lines if "no longer re-checks" is read to cover them.
- **A coarse export is written, not refused.** That is the decision's "does not re-admit". The record carries the cost.
- **The stamp, not the uncertainty entity, holds the record** for an export without a receiver profile, so the header rule T8 left alone stays as it was.

## Later

- The native tolerant admission of a file that records nothing reads gaps its own way and not through the gap rule for imports. See "Reopening and the gap rule for imports".
- The tolerant import's own STEP export (`TolerantImport::export_step`) measures its u/v correspondences when it writes, and its files carry no record, so reopening one measures again. It was not part of the measured cost in the decisions.
- Per-entity tolerances in the stamp would keep a tight entity tight across a reopen.
- The recorded pass still samples 17 stations of each 3D edge and places untrimmed ends by projection: 8.0M of the rim's 11.5M.
