# 3.0A copy tagging

Copy tagging of [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.4 (Q8). Every edge use has two copies of its curve: the 3D curve, and the face copy that the [edge-gap checker](3.0A-edge-gap-checker.md) lifts through the surface. The tag says how the two are related. The types are in `crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical.rs`, and [the interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-20-copy-tags-on-edge-uses.md) lists what callers see.

## The tag

`CopyTag` is `Independent` or `Derived { from, rule, bound }`.

- `Independent`: the two copies were made separately. Fitted, traced, projected, read from a file, or edited on one side.
- `Derived`: one copy was made from the other by a rule whose gap has a known bound. `from` names the authoritative copy (`Edge3d` or `Carrier`), `rule` names the rule, and `bound` is the largest distance the rule allows, in millimetres. An exact rule has bound zero.

The field is on `NumericalTrim` (the two u/v ends of a use) and on `NumericalNativeTrim` (a retained u/v curve). Neither type has a default for it, so every place that builds one states the tag. Where a use has a retained u/v curve, that curve is the face copy admission reads, and its tag is the one that counts.

## Who sets a derived tag

Today one rule has producers, `BlendRow`, in three places. Each has a seeded property test that runs the audit below.

| Producer | Where | Property test |
|---|---|---|
| Fillet between two spline faces | `fillet_numerical.rs`, `build_impl` | `the_rails_of_a_numerical_fillet_are_rows_of_its_blend_face` (`tests/m11_spline_fillet.rs`) |
| Rolled edge strip | `fillet_rolling_body.rs`, `cap_strip` | `the_rails_of_a_rolled_edge_are_rows_of_their_blend_face` |
| Rolled network with vertex patches | `fillet_rolling_vertex_body.rs`, `build` | `the_rails_of_a_rolled_network_are_rows_of_their_blend_faces` |

The rule: a rail of a blend face is a border row of the blend surface's control net, with the surface's own knots and weights along it, and the surface is clamped across. So the surface's parameter line at that border is the rail, and the bound is zero. The tag is `Derived { from: Carrier, rule: BlendRow, bound: 0 }`, on the rail's use of the blend face and on its u/v line there. The rail's other use, on the support face, is a fitted curve and stays `Independent`.

The property tests draw their sizes and radii from a seed. `CATALLAX_TAG_SEED` sets it. Without it the clock does, and the test prints the seed it used.

Section 4.4 names two more producers. Neither sets a tag.

- Analytic line and circle embeddings stay `Independent`, by the owner's ruling of 20 November 2026. Their u/v ends are 128-bit roundings of angles that are irrational in general, so no rule gives a bound without evaluating the gap, and a bound computed at construction would cost about 880 work units per use where the closed form of the checker's step 1 costs 8 to 14. Admission measures them by that closed form. `CopyRule` has no variant for them.
- U/v curves built by exact composition. `CopyRule::ExactComposition` exists. No code builds such a curve on a curved face today. The one exact composition in the tree projects a spline onto a plane (`hole/cap_ring.rs`), and a plane-projected use stays measured.

Other code builds parameter lines of a surface as edges: the boolean partition, the cylinder wall of a hole, the general sweep, and three places in `topology/src/numerical/surface.rs`. Section 4.4 does not name them, so they stay `Independent`.

## What admission does

`edge_gap::stored_sample` reads the tag of each use.

- `Independent`: the checker measures the use and stores 1.1 times the gap plus its measurement error (rule T4), under `trim.edge-surface-distance`.
- `Derived`: nothing is measured. The record holds the stated bound, with no margin, under `trim.edge-surface-derived-bound`. Rule T4's margin is for fresh measurements only. The two names keep a bound from being read as a measurement. The body's limit still applies, so a bound over the limit refuses with `numbers.numerical-tolerance-exceeded`, and a negative bound refuses with `numerical.invalid-definition`.
- A circle or a spline projected on a plane is measured whatever its tag says. That reading is the only check that the 3D curve lies on the plane.

## What resets a tag

A tag is a statement by the code that built both copies. Code that takes a use over from a body and changes either copy sets the tag back to `Independent`, unless one exact operation applies to both copies and keeps the bound. `NumericalDefinition::reset_copy_tags` resets every use of a definition.

Every path that takes uses over from a body:

| Path | What it does |
|---|---|
| `NumericalBody::replace_surface` | Resets the uses and the retained curves of the face whose surface changes. |
| `NumericalBody::rebuild`, `rebuild_with_native_trims` | The tags of the edited definition are the caller's, so the rebuild checks them. A use keeps a derived tag only when the body it starts from has a use at the same face, loop and place with the same tag, and both copies are the same definition there: the face surface, the sense and u/v ends, and the edge's curve and parameter range. A retained u/v curve keeps its tag only when its use keeps the same tag and the body retained the same curve with that tag. Every other derived tag is reset and its use measured. |
| `rebuild_with_sampled_carriers` | The producer path: the three fillets publish through it after resetting every use they take over, and tag only the blend face they built. It takes tags as stated, as `build` does, and the audit checks each producer's rule. |
| `NumericalBody::repair_vertex`, `numerical::sew` | Move or merge vertices only. Neither copy of any use changes, so tags stay. |
| The three fillets above | Reset every use of the body they start from, then tag the new blend face. A fillet moves ends and replaces uses on the faces it touches and does not show for each earlier derived copy that its rule still holds. So only the newest blend face of a body is tagged. |
| The axis edit of a numerical prism (`features/src/numerical.rs`, `deform`) | Resets. The map scales distances along one axis, so a stated bound does not hold after it. |
| STEP export, all three writers | Reset before the check of the written values. STEP carries no tag, and the writer rounds both copies. |

Holes, booleans and sweeps build their uses from the exact model or from their own recipe, not from a tagged body, and set `Independent`.

Three more facts from section 4.4:

- A stored body carries no tag. The field is skipped when a body is written, and reads back as `Independent`. The handover format is unchanged. A rebuild from the recipe sets the tags again.
- An imported body has every copy `Independent`.
- A rigid motion of both copies keeps a tag. No such path takes a tagged body today.

## The audit

A wrong tag ships a gap nobody measured. `numerical::audit_derived_copies(body, dec)` runs the full check on every derived copy of a body, the same reading admission gives an independent one, and returns one row per copy with the stated bound, the measured gap and the measurement error. A row holds when the measured gap is at most the bound plus the measurement error. The property test of each producer asserts that every row holds, so CI fails when a producer's rule stops being true.

`tests/numerical_copy_tags.rs` has the case the audit exists for: a cylinder wall 0.05 mm too wide, tagged as exact, is admitted, and the audit reports each tagged copy over 0.04 mm outside its bound.

## Effect on work

The saving is small, as section 4.4 says: the checks skipped are cheap. Charged work of four existing fillet tests, before (admission of the third part) and after:

| Test | Before | After | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Twelve rolled edges of a block, closed body | 49,079,058 | 49,046,770 | 32,288 |
| Fillet on the smooth loft, radius 1/10 | 19,560,056 | 19,534,905 | 25,151 |
| The same with a radius edit | 45,516,321 | 45,489,253 | 27,068 |
| One rolled edge, caps in the original plane domains | 16,037,534 | 16,034,668 | 2,866 |

Each blend face has two rails, so the saving per rail is about 1,350 units on the rolled block, about 1,430 on the single rolled edge and about 12,600 on the loft.
