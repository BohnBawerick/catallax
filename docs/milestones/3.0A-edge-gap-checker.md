# 3.0A edge-gap checker

The A+ checker of [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.2 (Q6). It measures the largest distance between an edge's 3D curve and its face copy lifted through the surface. The code is `crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/gap`. It is measured against the [known-answer edges](3.0A-known-answer-edges.md).

This note covers the checker alone. Admission still uses its old sampling. The replacement, copy tagging and the tolerance rules come in later parts of 3.0A.

## Method

Each step names its source in the code. The summary:

1. **Local frame.** One rational point near the edge is subtracted from every coordinate, and one rational u/v point from the parameters of planes and cylinders, before anything is rounded to binary64. This is rule T1's exact shift. Frames, radii and plane origins are built once as 128-bit balls and rounded once.
2. **Straight residual.** A line against a face copy whose lift is also straight has its largest gap at an end. Two evaluations. This is the closed form for a line on a plane or along a generator of a cylinder or cone (OpenCASCADE `ElementaryMaximum`).
3. **Breaks.** The edge is cut at the knots of the 3D curve, the knots of the face copy, every crossing of the face copy with a surface knot line, seam or pole line, the surface's domain border, and any parameter the builder hints. Crossings come from the Bezier form of the face copy on each piece (blossoming) and recursive halving with the variation diminishing test (Lane and Riesenfeld 1981). The seams a face copy crosses are counted before any is listed, and the work of finding breaks is charged as it is spent. An edge with more breaks than its evaluation allowance could sample refuses with `numerical.gap-work-cap` before a sample is taken.
4. **Samples.** Each piece gets `max(17, 3 x composed degree)` points, made even, at Chebyshev points of the first kind, plus both ends. The composed degree is the larger of the 3D curve's degree and the face copy's degree times the surface's degree sum, the OpenCASCADE rule.
5. **Tail.** The Chebyshev coefficients of the residual on each piece come from discrete orthogonality. The top third estimates what the samples leave unresolved. If four times that tail exceeds a tenth of the largest gap seen so far, the piece is split at an existing sample and both halves are sampled again. The tail only adds points. A piece that is still loud at the narrowest width that can be split refuses with `numerical.gap-unresolved`, and an edge that spends its evaluation allowance refuses with `numerical.gap-work-cap`. The allowance covers every evaluation of the edge: the five closing probes of the rounding allowance are set aside before the first sample, and a first plan larger than the allowance refuses before sampling. In both cases a peak may have been missed and cannot be bounded, so no tolerance comes out.
6. **Polish.** Every local maximum of the samples is bracketed by its neighbours and refined with Brent's `localmin` on the squared gap.
7. **Result.** The largest value, its parameter, a rounding allowance, a search uncertainty, a domain exit if there is one, the first plan, every split with its reason, and the evaluation count.

All arithmetic is IEEE basic operations and square roots in one thread. Sine and cosine come from a Cody-Waite reduction and Taylor sums written in the module, so no library function can differ between machines. The same bits come out on every machine and at any thread count. The receipt of the fourteen edges is identical, digit for digit and evaluation for evaluation, on the x86_64 Linux work PC and on an arm64 Mac.

## Two error figures, kept apart

`allowance_mm` is arithmetic only. It is the sum of three estimates at the reported point: the rounding of the two evaluations (an operation count times the roundoff times the size of the active controls), the face copy's rounding carried through the surface's rate of change, and the parameter's resolution times the gap's slope there.

`search_mm` is what the search itself may have left: four times the coefficient tail of the least resolved piece. Twice for the interpolation bound (Trefethen 2013, chapter 4), twice because the true maximum can sit one interpolation error above the interpolant's while the polished value sits one below. A piece is split until this figure fits inside the storage margin, so the margin is what covers it.

The reported gap is `raw_mm + allowance_mm`. Rule T4's stored tolerance is 1.1 times that, rounded up. `search_mm` is reported but not added: on every known-answer edge it is at most 1e-5 of the gap.

## Where this departs from section 4.2

Three choices differ from the letter of the decision. Each keeps its intent.

- **First-kind points, not the 17, 33, 65 grids.** The decision asks for Chebyshev points offset from the fitter's own check points. The 17-point grid includes the middle of the piece, which is the fitter's fourth eighth. With an even count of first-kind points no interior point is a rational share of its piece (Niven's theorem), so none can coincide with a builder that checks at eighths, sixteenths or thirds. The ends are sampled separately. A test checks this for every even count from 18 to 162.
- **Split in two, not double in place.** A loud piece is cut at an existing sample and each half gets the piece's point count. A narrow feature stays in one half, so the cost grows with depth and not with the square of the point count. The tail transform stays at the piece's own size.
- **The reported gap is raw plus allowance.** See the known-answer note.

## The three guards

1. **New edges every run.** `a_plus_finds_the_hidden_bump_on_fresh_edges` draws 40 edges from a new seed: 32 from the four frozen families of the known-answer note and 8 from the smooth surface family beside them. Every verdict must pass, with one stated exception for the steep surface family (below). I ran it 300 times, 12,000 edges, with no failure.
2. **Point count from shape only.** `PieceShape::points` takes three degrees and nothing else. `the_sample_plan_depends_on_shape_only` compares each fixed and fresh edge with a twin scaled by 7/3 and shifted: the pieces and points are equal, the method is equal, the twin's raw value is the scale times the original's within the two allowances, and every split carries a recorded reason. The only reason today is a loud tail.
3. **A source for every number.** `every_constant_in_the_checker_names_its_source` reads the three source files. Every constant must have a doc comment that says `Source:`, and no number other than 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 0.5 may appear outside a constant. Three constants are labelled as judgement: the per-edge evaluation cap, the root-finding depth and the step limit of Brent's method.

## What the seeded edges found

The seeded run is required to pass, and it earned its place. It turned up three things the fixed edges did not.

- **Knot rounding on narrow spans.** On a span 5e-7 wide at parameter 2.19 the rounded knots are known to one part in 1e9 of the span. The raw value came out 4.7e-13 mm short of a 4.5e-3 mm truth, more than the allowance then claimed. The evaluators now keep each knot as a binary64 value plus its exact remainder and take a difference as `(t - value) - remainder`, which is exact in its first step for nearby knots (Sterbenz' lemma). A unit test shows a hat 2^-40 wide evaluated to rounding with the remainders and off by more than 1e-3 without them.
- **Parameter resolution.** A maximum can sit half a unit in the last place away from any binary64 parameter. The allowance now includes that distance times the gap's slope near the reported point.
- **One frozen family is steep.** The surface family builds its base patch from independent random heights of 1 mm at every control, including the controls inside the hidden bump's knot cluster, which can be 2^-20 wide. That is a surface with slopes of millions of millimetres per unit parameter. The checker finds every bump there, but an honest allowance for the face copy's rounding, 7e-16 times that slope, can exceed a thousandth of the smallest truths, and the oracle has a rule that an allowance may not. The generator and its random stream are unchanged from the merged baseline, so a seed replays the same edges. A steep-family row that misses only that rule is held to the done-when line of the decisions instead (reported at least the true gap, at most 1.1 times it) and is listed in its own receipt, `known-answers-fresh-a-plus-steep.txt`. In the 300 runs that was 75 of 2,400 steep-family edges. Every other family, and every other rule on the steep family, must pass as before. The smooth family has the same bump on a patch whose heights follow a random quadratic; none of its 2,400 edges needed the exception.

## Open items from section 10, now measured

**The gain of A+ over plain sampling plus polish.** On the 22 fixed cases and on every seeded edge, no piece needed a split. Every piece there is polynomial or rational of modest degree, and the point count already resolves it. So on the known-answer set the tail adds nothing to what is found; it costs the transform and no evaluations. Its use shows on surfaces with no degree. `a_loud_tail_splits_a_piece_until_it_is_resolved` puts a degree-23 face copy on a cylinder, where the plan gives one piece of 18 points: the tail triggers four splits, 494 evaluations, and the result agrees with a dense sweep of exact values.

**The cost of a binary64 evaluation.** Edge 12 takes 4.3 ms for 14,109 evaluations on the work PC in a single-threaded run, about 300 ns each with the lifting of 1,036 surface controls included. Edge 1, on a plane, takes about 170 ns each. The 100 to 300 ns guess holds.

## Limits

- The checker finds maxima that its samples can bracket. A feature narrower than the spacing of 18 points on its piece, with no knot and no tail signal, is missed. Every sampled method has such a width (decisions, section 9.6).
- The allowance is an estimate from operation counts, not an enclosure. Nothing here is escalated to 128-bit arithmetic. Escalation near a cap belongs to the admission step.
- A circle on a plane or on its own cylinder goes through the sampled path, about 25 evaluations. A closed form would take four.
- Curves without a parameter evaluator (intersection curves, patch sections, patch pairs) are refused by name, as admission refuses them today.
