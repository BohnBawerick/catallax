# 3.0A edge-gap checker

The A+ checker of [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.2 (Q6). It measures the largest distance between an edge's 3D curve and its face copy lifted through the surface. The code is `crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/gap`. It is measured against the [known-answer edges](3.0A-known-answer-edges.md).

Numerical admission measures every edge use with it (see [the interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-20-admission-measures-edge-gaps.md)). Copy tagging and the tolerance rules come in later parts of 3.0A.

## Method

Each step names its source in the code. The summary:

1. **Local frame.** One rational point near the edge is subtracted from every coordinate, and one rational u/v point from the parameters of planes and cylinders, before anything is rounded to binary64. This is rule T1's exact shift. Frames, radii and plane origins are built once as 128-bit balls and rounded once.
2. **Closed forms.** A line against a face copy whose lift is also straight has its largest gap at an end: two evaluations, for a line on a plane or along a generator of a cylinder or cone. A circle or an ellipse against a lift that is one too, at the same angular rate, has a residual `O + A cos t + B sin t`. Its stationary points are the roots of a quartic in the tangent of the half angle, found in Bernstein form by halving, and the gap is read there and at the ends. That covers a circle over a plane and a circle on a surface of revolution whose copy keeps its v and turns with the edge. Both are the pairs of OpenCASCADE's `ElementaryMaximum`.
3. **Breaks.** The edge is cut at the knots of the 3D curve, the knots of the face copy, every crossing of the face copy with a surface knot line, seam or pole line, the surface's domain border, and any parameter the builder hints. Crossings come from the Bezier form of the face copy on each piece (blossoming) and recursive halving with the variation diminishing test (Lane and Riesenfeld 1981). The seams a face copy crosses are counted before any is listed, and the work of finding breaks is charged as it is spent.
4. **Samples.** Each piece gets `max(17, 3 x composed degree)` points, made even, at Chebyshev points of the first kind, plus both ends. Every piece gets the full count, however narrow it is. The composed degree is the larger of the 3D curve's degree and the face copy's degree times the surface's degree sum, the OpenCASCADE rule.
5. **Tail.** The Chebyshev coefficients of the residual on each piece come from discrete orthogonality. The top third estimates what the samples leave unresolved. While four times that tail exceeds a tenth of the largest gap seen so far, the points of the piece are doubled: a new grid of twice the count is sampled, and the earlier samples stay in the search. The tail only adds points. Doubling stops when the tail is quiet or when binary64 has no room for a finer grid between the ends.
6. **Polish.** Every local maximum of the samples is bracketed by its neighbours and refined with Brent's `localmin` on the squared gap.
7. **Error.** The measurement error is the binary64 rounding estimate at the reported point plus what the last tail says the samples left unresolved. Nothing is escalated to higher precision here. Escalation near a cap belongs to the admission step.
8. **Result.** The largest value, its parameter, the measurement error, a domain exit if there is one, the first plan, every doubling with its reason, and the evaluation count. Rule T4's stored tolerance is 1.1 times the value plus its error, rounded up.

An edge that would spend more than its work allowance refuses with `numerical.gap-work-cap`. The allowance counts everything that grows with the edge: one unit per break, per cut line a piece is tested against and per halving of that test while planning, one per residual evaluation, one per sample and mode pair of a tail transform, and one per domain probe. Every unit is taken before its work is done, in planning as in sampling, so the measurement stops where the allowance runs out, and the search starts with what planning left. The five closing probes of the rounding estimate are set aside before the first sample, and an edge with more breaks, or a larger first plan, than the allowance can pay for refuses before a sample is taken. The same units are charged to the operation's work. The only other refusal is `numerical.gap-unsupported`, for a curve or surface the checker cannot evaluate.

All arithmetic is IEEE basic operations and square roots in one thread. Sine and cosine come from a Cody-Waite reduction and Taylor sums written in the module, so no library function can differ between machines. The same bits come out on every machine and at any thread count.

## The measurement error

`error_mm` has two parts, added.

The rounding part is the sum of three estimates at the reported point: the rounding of the two evaluations (an operation count times the roundoff times the size of the active controls), the face copy's rounding carried through the surface's rate of change, and the parameter's resolution times the gap's slope there.

The search part is four times the coefficient tail of the least resolved piece, read on its last grid. Twice for the interpolation bound (Trefethen 2013, chapter 4), twice because the true maximum can sit one interpolation error above the interpolant's while the polished value sits one below. This is the term the research note behind rule T4 asks for (`docs/research/cp2-grill/tolerance-numbers-research.md`, section 5, item 2): the tolerance is 1.1 times the gap plus the measurement error, and the measurement error includes what the search may have left.

The reported gap is `raw_mm + error_mm`. The stored tolerance is 1.1 times that.

## Section 4.2, step by step

- Step 1 asks for closed forms for a line or a circle on a plane and for conic pairs. All three are here. A pair is taken as a conic pair only when both lifted curves are circles or ellipses at the same rate, decided on the exact values; a circle against a straight copy on a plane, or against a copy that turns at another rate, is sampled.
- Step 2, imports whose copies run at different speeds, belongs to the import gap rule, a later part of 3.0A.
- Step 4 asks for points offset from the fitter's own check points. The fitter checks at eighths of a span. A Chebyshev grid with an odd count has the middle of the piece, which is the fourth eighth, so the count is made even: 17 becomes 18. No point of an even first-kind grid is a rational share of its piece (Niven's theorem). A test checks every even count from 18 to 162 against thirds and sixty-fourths.
- The paragraph after the steps says Chebyshev point sets nest (17, 33, 65), so doubling reuses every point. The nested grids are the ones that contain the middle, so that line and step 4 cannot both hold. Here a doubling takes a fresh grid and keeps the earlier samples in the search.
- Step 7's escalation is in admission, and it is the only place the precision rises. When a stored tolerance is over the body's limit and would be at or under it without the binary64 rounding error of the measurement, the gap at the reported parameter is enclosed at 128 bits and that value is stored. What the samples left unresolved stays in the tolerance.
- Steps 3, 5, 6 and 8 are as written. Step 5 doubles the points of the piece.

## The three guards

1. **New edges every run.** `a_plus_finds_the_hidden_bump_on_fresh_edges` draws 40 edges from a new seed: 32 from the four frozen families of the known-answer note and 8 from the smooth surface family beside them. Every verdict must pass. I ran it 300 times, 12,000 edges, with no failure.
2. **Point count from shape only.** `PieceShape::points` takes three degrees and nothing else. `the_sample_plan_depends_on_shape_only` compares each fixed and seeded edge with a twin scaled by 7/3 and shifted: the pieces and points are equal, the method is equal, the twin's raw value is the scale times the original's within the two errors, and every doubling carries a recorded reason. The only reason is a loud tail.
3. **A source for every number.** `every_constant_in_the_checker_names_its_source` reads the three source files. Every constant must have a doc comment that says `Source:`, and no number other than 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 0.5 may appear outside a constant. Three constants are labelled as judgement: the per-edge evaluation cap, the root-finding depth and the step limit of Brent's method.

## What the seeded edges found

The seeded run turned up two things the fixed edges did not.

- **Knot rounding on narrow spans.** On a span 5e-7 wide at parameter 2.19 the rounded knots are known to one part in 1e9 of the span. The raw value came out 4.7e-13 mm short of a 4.5e-3 mm truth, more than the error then claimed. The evaluators now keep each knot as a binary64 value plus its exact remainder and take a difference as `(t - value) - remainder`, which is exact in its first step for nearby knots (Sterbenz' lemma). A unit test shows a hat 2^-40 wide evaluated to rounding with the remainders and off by more than 1e-3 without them.
- **Parameter resolution.** A maximum can sit half a unit in the last place away from any binary64 parameter. The error now includes that distance times the gap's slope near the reported point.

One frozen family is steep. The surface family builds its base patch from independent random heights of 1 mm at every control, including the controls inside the hidden bump's knot cluster, which can be 2^-20 wide. That is a surface with slopes of millions of millimetres per unit parameter, and the face copy's own rounding, carried through that slope, shows in the error. The largest error I saw on that family was 4.4% of the true gap, on a bump of 3.9e-8 mm. The pass rule allows 10%, so every such edge passed, but a seed can exist that lands above it. The receipt prints the seed, so such a run can be replayed. The generator and its random stream are unchanged from the merged baseline. The smooth family has the same bump on a patch whose heights follow a random quadratic; its largest error was 2.3e-5 of the true gap.

## Open items from section 10, now measured

**The gain of A+ over plain sampling plus polish.** On the 22 fixed cases and on all 12,000 seeded edges, no piece needed a doubling. Every piece there is polynomial or rational of modest degree, and the first grid already resolves it. So on the known-answer set the tail adds nothing to what is found. It costs the transform and no evaluations. Its use shows on surfaces with no degree. `a_loud_tail_doubles_the_points_of_a_piece_until_it_is_quiet` puts a degree-23 face copy on a cylinder, where the plan gives one piece of 18 points: the tail triggers two doublings, to 72 points and 508 evaluations in all, and the result agrees with a dense sweep of exact values.

**The cost of a binary64 evaluation.** Edge 12 takes 5.7 to 8.2 ms for 14,109 evaluations on the work PC in a single-threaded run with other jobs on the machine: 400 to 580 ns each, with the lifting of 1,036 surface controls and the tail transforms included. Edge 1, on a plane, takes 230 to 420 ns each. The 100 to 300 ns guess is about right on a plane and low on a bicubic surface.

## Limits

- The checker finds maxima that its samples can bracket. A feature narrower than the spacing of 18 points on its piece, with no knot and no tail signal, is missed. Every sampled method has such a width (decisions, section 9.6).
- The error is an estimate from operation counts and a coefficient tail, not an enclosure. The checker escalates nothing to 128-bit arithmetic. Admission does so in one case, a stored tolerance within the rounding error of the body's limit.
- Curves without a parameter evaluator (intersection curves, patch sections, patch pairs) are refused by name, as admission refused them before.
