# 3.0A known-answer edges

Roadmap item 3.0A starts with a test set, built before the new edge-gap checker so that the checker is measured against an oracle it did not shape. This note describes that oracle and what the old admission sampling read on it. The decision is Q11 and Q22 of the [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.1.

The code is the test-only module `crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/known_answers`. It holds no checker and changes no admission rule.

## What a case is

A case is one edge with two copies. The 3D copy is a line, a circle or a B-spline curve. The face copy is a u/v curve, or two u/v end values joined by a straight line, lifted through a plane, a cylinder or a spline carrier. The gap is the distance between the copies at the same edge parameter, and the truth is its maximum over the edge.

Every case is built so that the copies agree exactly except for one scalar displacement along a fixed direction. The gap is then that scalar, and its maximum is one of these.

- A rational number: a hat function's height, a Bernstein maximum `C(n,k) (k/n)^k (1-k/n)^(n-k)`, a polynomial value.
- The maximum of one B-spline basis function, or of two lifted neighbours, on a non-uniform knot vector. Such a bump rises once and falls once, so bisection on the sign of its exact derivative encloses the maximum to about 2^-120 of the edge.
- A closed form with a square root, an arctangent and a sine (edge 14), evaluated as a 128-bit ball.

Truths are rational enclosures `[lo, hi]`. The widest is edge 7b, whose face copy is shifted by the stored rational period and so differs from the circle by the radius times that period's distance from 2 pi.

The oracle checks itself without any checker. For each case it evaluates the exact definitions as 128-bit enclosures, confirms the truth is attained at its stated parameter, and confirms that no point of a dense sample exceeds it. The sample clusters around the stated parameter down to 2^-39 of the edge.

Three cases need a word more.

Edge 9. The kernel's evaluators refuse a parameter outside a spline's domain. A receiving system does not: it evaluates the end span's polynomial past the border. The truth is therefore the gap against that continuation, `h (21/20)^3`. The receipt records where a checker saw the face copy leave the domain; the rule does not judge it.

Edge 13. Three controls agree by rule: a line on its plane, a circle on its cylinder, and a boundary view of the carrier. The fourth stores an interior iso-curve as its own curve, so only a measurement can show the agreement. The true gap of all four is zero. A control passes when a checker reports about zero, which the rule below takes as at most 1e-7 mm.

Edge 14. With `tau = tan(alpha/2) = 1/3` the rational copy sits at angle `2 atan(tau x)` for `x = 2t - 1` and the face copy at `a x`. The difference is stationary at `x^2 = (2 tau / a - 1) / tau^2`. The gap is the chord `2 R sin(d/2)`.

## The pass rule

A checker reports a raw estimate and its own measurement error. The reported gap is their sum, before the storage margin, which is the quantity rule T4 multiplies by 1.1. `known_answer_holds` is the done-when line of 3.0A read on that one quantity, on both sides.

- The reported gap is at least the true gap.
- The reported gap is at most 1.1 times the true gap.
- A truth known as an enclosure counts at its upper end on the first line and at its lower end on the second, so its width never helps a checker.

Nothing else is judged. An earlier version of this module had three more rules (a cap on the allowance's share, the place of a domain exit, and a claim of exact zero). They were not in the decisions and are gone.

One case needs a number the line does not give. A zero-gap control has a true gap of zero, and 1.1 times zero is zero, which no binary64 measurement of a curved copy returns. A control passes when its reported gap is at most 1e-7 mm, the smallest value of rule T1's floor for binary64 geometry (decisions, section 4.3).

Why the sum and not the raw estimate alone. A binary64 estimate is a value the gap attains at some parameter, so it sits at or below the true maximum by its rounding error. The raw value cannot be required to reach the truth except by luck. The checker's bound on that error is part of what it reports, and it is the quantity the margin multiplies under rule T4.

The stored tolerance is in the receipt but takes no part in this rule. The storage rule (T4) is tested where it is built.

A checker under test receives an `EdgeGeometry`: the two copies, the surface and the edge parameters. The case name, the truth, its location, the expected domain exit and the zero classification stay in the harness. `accept` runs every fixed case with no filter and fails unless the rows match the 22 required case names in order and all pass.

## Guards against tuning

The first guard is in this module. `fresh::cases(seed, n)` builds new edges from four families: a Boehm-refined lift on a plane, a bump on one control of a random spline patch, a Bernstein bump inside one polynomial piece, and a height bump on a cylinder arc. Each has one hidden bump of known size and place. Degrees, knot vectors, bump widths (2^-3 to 2^-20 of the edge), sizes (1e-7 to 1e-2 mm), origins (up to 1e6 mm) and frames are drawn from the seed. The seed comes from the clock unless `CATALLAX_GAP_SEED` is set, and every receipt prints it. None of the four builders is used by the fixed edges.

These four families and their random stream are frozen, so a seed replays the edges the baseline was recorded with. A later family goes in its own module with its own stream. The first is `smooth::cases`: the surface family again, on a patch whose heights follow a random quadratic, so only the hidden bump is narrow.

The module also gives the second guard a handle. `fresh::twin` scales and shifts a case exactly. The twin has the same degrees, knots, weights and surface kind, and its truth is the scale times the original. A checker whose point count depends only on shape must plan the same points for both.

The third guard, a source for every method and constant, belongs to the checker's own code.

## Receipts

A receipt has one line per edge: the true gap, the raw estimate, the error allowance, the reported gap, the stored tolerance, the evaluations, the charged work and the verdict. Its first line names the checker and the seed. Set `CATALLAX_GAP_RECEIPT` to a directory to keep the files.

```sh
CATALLAX_GAP_RECEIPT=/tmp/receipts cargo test --release -p catallax-topology --lib known_answers -- --nocapture
```

## What the old sampling read

This section is a record. Admission now measures each edge use with the [edge-gap checker](3.0A-edge-gap-checker.md), and the baseline code left with the sampling it measured. The numbers below were taken at commit 40a6043e, the last with both.

Admission had two sampling rules. Ordinary trims and the edges of declared numerical carriers got nine equal locations. Retained native u/v curves got the eighths of every clipped knot span of the two curves. The baseline took its locations from the admission code and evaluated the gap as admission did. A test showed the values equal `native::samples`, sample for sample, on an analytic face.

Both rules read the four zero-gap controls as about 1e-36 mm, the width of a 128-bit enclosure.

The nine-point rule meets the known-answer rule on the zero-gap controls only. The knot-span rule adds edges 1 and 6, where the peak is on one of its sample locations. Neither sees edges 2 and 11 at all. Both come within 1% on the smooth bumps and still fall short, because neither moves off its sample locations. Edge 9 is refused by name (`shapes.bspline-domain`), which is the kernel's evaluator and not a finding of the sampling.

Edge 12 is the cost case. The knot-span rule spends 8.13M work on one copy of one 256-span edge, 2,049 evaluations in 128-bit arithmetic, and reads 0.997 of the gap.

The knot-span rule existed for native u/v curves only, so the baseline ran it on those cases.
