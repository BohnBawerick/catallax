# 3.0A tolerance rules T0 to T8

Tolerance rules T0 to T8 of [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.3 (Q7), with the export receiver profile. The numbers are in `crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/tolerance.rs`, one function or constant per number, each naming its rule. [The interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-20-tolerance-rules.md) lists what callers see.

## The numbers

| Rule | Number | Where |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | `eps(R) = max(1e-7 mm, 1e-11 x R)`; warn above 1 km | `floor_mm`, `floor_minimum_mm`, `floor_ratio`, `body_size_warning_mm` |
| T2 | target `max(1e-6 mm, 10 x eps)`, tightened to `L/100`; cap `max(1e-5 mm, 100 x eps)` | `own_fitted`, `fitted_target_minimum_mm`, `fitted_cap_minimum_mm` |
| T3 | 0.01 mm for edges and faces, 0.02 mm for vertices, opt-in up to 0.1 mm | `import_cap_mm`, `import_vertex_cap_mm`, `import_opt_in_limit_mm`, `import_cap_for` |
| T4 | 1.1 x (gap + measurement error), fresh measurements only | unchanged since the third part: `gap::MARGIN`, `edge_gap::margin` |
| T5 | error if `t_a + t_b > d/2`, warn if `> d/5`, boxes inflated by `5t` | `pair_verdict`, `PAIR_ERROR_DIVISOR`, `PAIR_WARNING_DIVISOR`, `PAIR_INFLATION` |
| T7 | warn above 10 x the input | `grew`, `GROWTH_FACTOR` |
| T8 | NX 0.01 mm, CATIA 0.001 mm; bands at 10 x and 4 x | `ReceiverProfile::nx`, `ReceiverProfile::catia`, `RECEIVER_WIDE_BAND`, `RECEIVER_NARROW_BAND` |

Each has a unit test that reads it back (`tolerance::tests`).

"The cap that applies is always the smaller of the class cap and the local term." The class cap is the body's limit, `NumericalOptions::tolerance_mm`, as it was. The local term is rule T5: a pair whose tolerances add up to more than half their distance refuses. On a part with no small feature no pair is found, and the class cap governs.

## Where each rule acts

**T0.** Exact bodies store no tolerance and are not touched: nothing on the exact path reads these rules. T1's floor and size warning are for binary64 geometry, which in Catallax means a numerical body.

**T1.** Admission measures every body in a frame centred on the box of its vertices and edge-defining points, reached by an exact rational shift (`BodyFrame`). `R` is half the box's largest side. Every entity's tolerance is at least `eps(R)`, and a body whose box is larger than 1 km on a side is admitted with a `tolerance.t1-body-over-1-km` warning. The edge-gap checker already works in a local frame per edge (the third part); the tolerance rules read distances between entities in the body's frame. The box holds a circle's whole centre-and-radius square, not only its arc, so `R` can be larger than the body; that only raises the floor.

**T2.** Own fitted geometry. The recipe evaluation of a numerical fillet (`evaluate_numerical_fillet.rs`) and the CLI's numerical export of an exact body (`numerical_step.rs`) take `eps` in the frame of the exact body's vertices, fit to the target and admit under the cap: 1e-6 mm and 1e-5 mm for any part under 10 m, where the code had 1e-7 mm for both. The fillet target is tightened to `L/100` where the source body has two entities closer than `100 x target` (`features_within`, below), never under `eps`. The rolled fillets publish under the class cap of the body they cut. The loft cut (`numerical_loft_step.rs`) keeps 1e-7 mm; see Limits. The spline fillet API (`fillet::numerical::build`) keeps its rule that the stated tolerance is also the result's limit where it is under the source's; the recipe states the target there.

**T3.** Imports. Numerical import admits through `numerical::admit_import`. Every entity is measured as before; the file's declared uncertainty is read by the STEP parser and used by nothing, which is what "a hint" leaves room for. A caller's cap of 0.01 mm is the automatic one; a larger one is the user's opt-in, refused above 0.1 mm (`numerical.import-opt-in`, and `import.numerical-tolerance` at the byte entry). When a stored tolerance is over the cap, the face copies over it are refitted first from their 3D curves (below), on a probe admitted at 0.1 mm, and the definition is admitted again under the caller's cap with the refitted copies. What is still over refuses with the existing `numbers.numerical-tolerance-exceeded`, and the detail says the user may opt in. The caps go by class (`tolerance::import_options`): a vertex takes 0.02 mm, edges and faces 0.01 mm, an opt-in raises every class to it, and a caller's stricter cap holds for all. `NumericalOptions::vertex_tolerance_mm` carries the vertex cap; admission holds each record of an edge end's distance to its vertex to it, and every other record (edge-face gaps, u/v endpoint and join samples) to the class cap (`tolerance::check_by_class`). The evidence states the larger of the two. A stored body keeps the vertex cap in its tolerance report. The tolerant import's growth checks a vertex against 0.02 mm and an edge against 0.01 mm (`TolerantBody::cap_mm`), and its numerical admission takes the automatic caps by class: a `maximum_mm` that healing raised above 0.01 mm is no user's opt-in.

**T4.** As built in the third part. The floor of T1 is applied to the entity tolerances of the report, not to the evidence records, which keep rule T4's value.

**T5.** Admission builds a bounding-box tree over every vertex, edge and face, each box widened by 5 times the entity's tolerance (`tolerance/pairs.rs`). Only pairs whose widened boxes overlap and that share no topology are measured: two different vertices, a vertex and an edge that does not end at it, two edges with no common vertex, and a vertex or an edge and a face whose boundary it does not touch. An error refuses with `numerical.tolerance-pair`; a warning is `tolerance.t5-pair-close` in the report. Distances are found as the checker finds gaps: the sample count of section 4.2 step 4 on each piece between knots, and Brent's method on every sampled local minimum, in binary64 in the body's frame. The tree is split at the median of the box centres along the longest axis (Ericson 2005).

A face of a numerical body stores no tolerance: its surface is the authoritative copy and the gap lives on its edges. So a pair with a face has `t_face = 0`. Its distance is to the face's interior: the foot of a point on the surface, seeded from a 9 by 9 grid on the face's u/v box and found by Gauss-Newton, counted where it lies inside the face's loops (read as u/v polygons, a retained u/v curve at nine points). An edge is read at its samples and polished by Brent's method. Nearer the boundary, the face's own edges and vertices are the pair. A face's box is its edges' box for a plane, its controls' box for a spline, and the box of its local frame's extent over its v range for a surface of revolution. Two faces both have `t = 0`, so rule T5 cannot fire on them and they are not paired; a thin wall shows as the edges of one side against the other side's interior. A short edge shows as its two vertices, a sliver face as two of its edges.

The search is charged as work: every box test of the tree, every sample of an edge, every grid point and foot step on a face, every Brent evaluation, and the lookups that pair entities with their tolerances. Edges and faces are sampled only when a pair needs them, so a part with no candidate pair samples nothing. The operation's allowance is the hard cap: a search it exhausts refuses with `numbers.operation-budget`.

**T6.** No other rule makes a short edge or a sliver invalid. Admission had none; the exact STEP writer's regularisation of short edges is on the exact path (T0).

**T7.** Every continuation of a numerical body (`rebuild`, `rebuild_with_native_trims`, `rebuild_with_sampled_carriers`, `replace_surface`, `repair_vertex`) measures every use again (rule T4, never stacked), so a tolerance comes down when its gap does. Each entity of the result is then compared with its input: its own tolerance before the operation, or, for a new entity, the largest tolerance of the input. Above 10 times, a `tolerance.t7-growth-over-10x` warning carries the input, the output, the cap and the headroom under the cap. An edge the input already had that rose over 10 times has its face copies refitted, aiming under 10 times its input; where the refit gets there, the body is admitted again with the new copies. A new edge was fitted by the operation that made it and is not fitted again.

The trigger is the decisions' own: more than 10 times. It stays a warning. Its absolute values travel with it, because the ratio alone says little when the input is near zero. The chain test below shows both sides.

**T8.** `catallax_step::numerical::build` takes `NumericalStepOptions::receiver` (`ReceiverProfile::nx()`, `::catia()`, or `::custom`). Before writing, it refits the face copies of every use whose stored tolerance is over the receiver's, keeping each refit that gets under it; lists the entities still over it; lists every pair of entities that share no topology and are closer than 10 times the receiver's tolerance, in the band under 10 times or under 4 times; and, for a part with a feature in the band under 4 times, offers the smallest scaled export (10, 100 or 1,000 times) that lifts every feature to 10 times the receiver's tolerance; and writes a global uncertainty, the largest entity tolerance of the source and of the check of the written values, rounded up. Without a profile the header has no uncertainty, as before. A scaled export (`NumericalStepOptions::scale`) writes the same millimetre numbers under the centimetre, decimetre or metre, so the receiver reads a part that many times larger, and stamps `scaled_export=x1000;written_values=millimetres`. The receiver reads a scaled file at its scale, so its limits apply there (reviewer hold on `83858ed4`): the refit aims under the receiver's tolerance divided by the scale, the feature search reaches 10 times the receiver's tolerance divided by the scale, and residuals and features are reported both in the model's millimetres and as the receiver reads them (`ReceiverResidual::receiver_tolerance_mm`, `ReceiverFeature::receiver_distance_mm`), with bands taken on the latter. Nothing is rounded twice. The receiver and its tolerance go in the stamp too.

## The refit

`gap/refit.rs`. A refit replaces an edge use's face copy with a u/v cubic in the edge's own parameter, so the gap stays a same-parameter gap (Q9 for own builds). At each node the 3D point's foot on the surface is found by Gauss-Newton from the old copy, and the foot's rate from a second foot a thousandth of a span along. Each span is the cubic Hermite piece of the two feet and rates (Farin, section 7.5); the nodes are the knots of the 3D curve with each piece cut into equal spans, doubling until the checker reads a stored tolerance under the target or the 256-span limit of the embedding fitter is reached. The end knots are the edge's exact parameters. The checker measures every candidate, and a refit is kept only where it reaches its target. A curve projected on a plane has no u/v copy, and a gap in the geometry (a 3D curve off its surface) is not something a copy can lower.

### The chain

`a_chain_reports_absolute_tolerances_and_headroom_at_every_stage`. The quarter starts with its wall on its edges, so every edge stores the floor. Then the wall moves out four times. The cap is 0.01 mm throughout.

| Stage | Wall radius | Largest edge before | Largest edge after | Ratio | Headroom under the cap | Warnings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | | 1e-7 mm (floor) | | | |
| 1 | 1.000001 | 1e-7 mm | 1.1e-6 mm | 11.0 | 0.0099989 mm | 4 edges |
| 2 | 1.000003 | 1.1e-6 mm | 3.3e-6 mm | 3.0 | 0.0099967 mm | none |
| 3 | 1.0005 | 3.3e-6 mm | 5.5e-4 mm | 166.7 | 0.00945 mm | 4 edges |
| 4 | 1.0009 | 5.5e-4 mm | 9.9e-4 mm | 1.8 | 0.00901 mm | none |

Stage 1 is the near-zero input: the ratio is 11 and the warning fires, while the absolute value is 1.1e-6 mm with 99.99% of the cap left. Stage 3 fires on a value 500 times larger with 94.5% left. Neither refuses: the cap is what refuses, and the trigger is the decisions' own. The refit runs on both warned stages and lowers nothing, because the gap is in the geometry; the warning stays, with its numbers.

## Choices to confirm

- **The floor applies to entity tolerances, not to evidence records.** A record keeps rule T4's value; the report raises it to `eps`.
- **A face stores no tolerance**, so a face pairs with vertices and edges at its interior, and two faces are not paired (both `t = 0`).
- **The T7 refit runs only on edges the input had.** A new edge was just fitted by its operation; its growth over the input's largest tolerance is reported, not refitted.
- **A refit is kept only where it reaches its target**: under the receiver's tolerance (T8), the cap (T3), or 10 times the input (T7).
- **The uncertainty is written for an export with a receiver profile**, where rule T8 puts it. A file without a profile keeps the header it had, so files and evidence written before are unchanged. Writing it for every numerical file is a one-line change if the owner reads T8 that way.
- **A scaled export changes the length unit, not the numbers.** Only factors 10, 100 and 1,000 exist, the SI prefixes between the millimetre and the metre.

## Limits

- The loft cut and the traced-intersection fitters inside the boolean crate keep 1e-7 mm. The loft cut's selection uses one value as fit target and limit. At rule T2's 1e-6 mm target, the three top cuts of the M10 regression (M10-004 to M10-006) answer at 17.6M work instead of refusing at the 20M allowance, which the slow regression requires them to do (`slow_m10_top_refusals.py`). That is a change of outcome for the owner to decide, so this part leaves the loft cut as it was.
- A body read back from a handover written before this layout has an empty report: no entity tolerances and no T7 comparison until it is rebuilt.
