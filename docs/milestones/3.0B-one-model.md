# 3.0B one model

Roadmap item 3.0B, one model, of [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.5 (Q12). This note covers the tolerance on the three entities, the order rule, the one checker, re-tightening and the fence in front of the exact boolean. What is still to come is listed at the end. [The interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-21-entity-tolerance.md) lists what callers change, and [the topology contract](../pieces/03-topology.md) holds the rules.

## What other kernels do

Parasolid puts tolerances on topology and stores geometry as it is. Jackson 1995, "Boundary representation modelling with local tolerances", is the source: an edge at default precision has one 3D curve, a tolerant edge also has a curve on each face inside a tube of its tolerance, and vertex tolerance >= edge tolerance >= face tolerance. Catallax follows that structure. The decisions name it, and [the research note](../research/cp2-grill/exact-tolerant-coexist.md) quotes the lines.

OpenCASCADE stores a binary64 tolerance on every vertex, edge and face (`myTolerance` of `BRep_TVertex`, `BRep_TEdge` and `BRep_TFace`), so it has no exact state. It keeps the same order by repair: `BRepLib::UpdateTolerances` raises values "with rule Tolerance(VERTEX)>=Tolerance(EDGE)>=Tolerance(FACE)" (`BRepLib.cxx`, `InternalUpdateTolerances`, read at tag V7_8_0). SMLib sets an edge's tolerance to twice its largest gap and a vertex's to twice the larger of its edge and face gaps (`SmBrep::ValidateAndUpdateTolerances`, as read for [the tolerance research](../research/cp2-grill/t-smarter-research.md)).

Catallax differs in two ways. An entity with no tolerance is exact, which is rule T0 of the decisions, so the field is optional and a tolerance of zero does not exist. And the checker reports a broken order as a fault where OpenCASCADE raises the value: a raised tolerance would be a number nobody measured, and the builder that made the entity is the one that can measure it.

## The field

`Vertex`, `Edge` and `Face` in `crates/catallax-topology/src/entity.rs` each have `tolerance: Option<Tolerance>`.

- `None` is exact. The owner settled the open sub-question of section 4.5 on 21 November 2026: rule T0 stands, and an exact entity has no floor.
- `Tolerance` wraps a rational length in millimetres that is greater than zero. `Tolerance::new` refuses zero and negative values, and so does the reader of a stored one. There is one spelling of exact.
- Geometry storage does not change. A point, a curve and a surface are stored as before, fitted ones as exact rationals.

A vertex's tolerance is how far its point may lie from the end of each of its edges' curves. An edge's is how far its curve may lie from its copy on each of its faces. A face's is how far its surface may lie from the surface it stands for. Nothing writes a face tolerance today.

## The order rule

Vertex >= edge >= face, with exact counting as zero. `Model::check` reports `topology.tolerance-order` for each pair that breaks it: a vertex against an edge that ends at it, an edge against a face that uses it, and a vertex loop's vertex against its face.

The rule runs one way. A tolerant edge forces its vertices to at least its own value. It never forces a face, so an exact face beside a tolerant edge stays exact, which is the decisions' line "An exact face never takes a tolerance. Only vertices and edges forced by a tolerant neighbour do". No code raises a face's tolerance because of a neighbour.

## The one checker

`Model::check` is the checker of exact, tolerant and mixed bodies. It reads entities, their tolerances and recorded facts. It measures nothing and takes no work allowance, as before.

For each pair that must meet (a vertex on its edge's curve, an edge's curve on its face's surface, a vertex loop's point on its face's surface) it asks for one record.

- Both entities exact: the `Incident` fact in the fact book, as before. `topology.missing-incidence` if it is absent.
- Either entity tolerant: the tolerance. The builder measured the distance and stored 1.1 times it on the entity (rule T4), and that value is the record, as a fact is the record of an exact builder's proof.

This is check once (3.0A step 5) applied to the checker: a tolerance is measured where the entity is made and trusted after that. `Model::check` cannot find a tolerance that is smaller than the true distance, as it cannot find a recorded fact that is false.

## Numerical bodies

A `NumericalBody` already held a `Model` body beside its tables. Three things changed.

1. Admission stores each vertex's and each edge's tolerance, the values of its tolerance report, on that model's entities. No face stores one: a face's surface is the authoritative copy, and the gap is on its edges. Every vertex of a numerical body is tolerant, because a tolerance is never under rule T1's floor. So is every edge, except one that is re-tightened to exact (below).
2. Admission's last step is `Model::check` on that model. The structure part of the check still runs first, on the same topology without tolerances, so a broken shell refuses before any distance is measured. Refusal order, refusal codes and counted work are as before.
3. `NumericalBody::topology_faults` returns `Model::check` of the body's model and replaces `exact_topology_faults`. It is empty for an admitted body. `NumericalBody::stored_tolerance` reads a vertex's or an edge's tolerance from the model by key.

So a numerical body's topology is a body of the one model type, checked by the one checker. `NumericalBody` is a view of that body with what the model does not hold: the keys, the face copies of each edge use, the copy tags, the evidence and the history.

The model stays private in this part. An operation on exact bodies does not yet keep tolerant entities out of the exact intersector, so a numerical body's model must not reach one until the next part adds that.

What the measuring half does is unchanged. The edge-gap checker, the tolerance rules T1 to T7 and the u/v loop checks read the definition's tables and run once, at admission. They produce the tolerances that `Model::check` reads.

### Older stored bodies

The body handover moves to format version 3. A version 1 or 2 record has no tolerance on any entity, which is right for an exact body. A numerical body read from such a record gets its model built again with tolerances (`restore_model_tolerances`): the values of its stored report, or, for a record older than the report, the largest tolerance its evidence records on every vertex and edge, and at least rule T1's fixed 1e-7 mm. A numerical body is never read back as exact.

## Copies

A copy that keeps an entity's geometry keeps its tolerance.

| Copy | Where |
|---|---|
| Isometry copy of a body | `transform.rs`. An isometry keeps every distance, so the value moves unchanged. |
| Journal replay | `model/journal.rs`. |
| Handover | the codecs in `entity.rs`. |
| A face cut into cells on its own surface | `imprinting.rs`, `imprinting/bezier.rs`, `numerical/loft.rs` in the Boolean crate. |
| STEP export's split of a face or an edge | `equator.rs`, `poles.rs`, `regularize.rs`, `cut.rs` in the STEP crate. |

Every other place that builds a vertex, an edge or a face writes `None`. Those are builders of exact geometry, and until the next part no tolerant entity reaches them.

Four of them rebuild entities from records that do not carry a tolerance yet. The next part must carry it there, or keep tolerant entities out, before a tolerant entity can reach them:

- `sewing.rs` in the Boolean crate, which assembles a result's vertices, edges and faces from `EdgeData` and `FaceData`, merges edges, and records an `Incident` fact for every pair it writes;
- `rational_atlas.rs` in the topology crate, which builds logical faces and edges from groups of chart faces;
- `wire.rs` in the STEP crate, which rebuilds a face from its wires;
- every site that makes a new vertex where it cuts an existing edge. A vertex on a tolerant edge needs at least the edge's tolerance, and `Model::check` reports `topology.tolerance-order` until it has one.

## The work cap of the edge-gap checker is one allowance for an edge

Section 4.2 step 8 says "A hard per-edge work cap refuses by name." The edge is the topological edge. The cap, `gap::EDGE_WORK`, is one million units, and every use of an edge draws on the same allowance.

3.0A gave each edge use an allowance of its own, so an edge between two faces could count up to twice the cap. The reviewer held that against the decisions line, and this part changes it. The number and the refusal's name are as before.

- An admission has one allowance per edge and a record of what was read of each use (`edge_gap::Accounts`). The uses are read in the definition's order, and each is given what the readings before it left of its edge's allowance. The accounts are indexed by the edge index the trims already hold, so nothing is sorted and no geometry is read twice.
- Every admission has them: an own build, a continuation, an import and the admission after a refit. An admission that is given none starts its own.
- In an import, admission's reading of a use, the geometric reading, the change of parameter and rule T3's refit all draw on the edge's allowance, as they drew on the use's before.
- The refits of rules T7 and T8 draw on the allowance of their use's edge and share accounts with the admission that follows, which uses the reading a kept refit took. Before, such a refit counted its feet without a limit and read each candidate under a cap of its own. `gap::refit::refit`, the entry without a limit, is gone.
- The audit of derived copies gives each edge one allowance for the audit.
- The import crate's own reader (`tolerant/admission/fit.rs`) reads a file's uses one at a time through `read_import_use`. It now keeps what each edge has counted (`EdgeAllowances`) and gives each use what its edge has left.

An edge whose uses together need more than its allowance refuses with `numerical.gap-work-cap`. Where they need less, every stored tolerance, every answer and all counted work are as before, with one exception: after a kept refit of rule T7 or T8 the admission no longer reads the new copy a second time, so that pass counts less work.

Every unit a reading counts is taken off its edge, also where the reading is refused or its use is found unread afterwards. The checker's entries add what they counted to their caller's count whether or not they finish (`measure_counted` and its siblings, and `UseDraw::draw_on`, which takes the count off the edge). The import crate's reader goes on after a use it cannot keep, so it carries the count on both outcomes (`Unread::work`) and takes it off before it decides to rebuild the use's trace (`EdgeAllowances::read`). The reviewer's second hold found the case: a new copy that leaves the charts of its surface after the rule had run. The edge's next use gets only what is left.

The import crate admits a file's bodies in rounds: as written, then with their boundaries repaired. Each round measures its own candidate of a body and gives every edge of it a fresh allowance, so a body that an edge refused by its cap would get a second allowance for that edge in the next round, and the repair round may read the edge through another carrier. The cap is hard, so that refusal ends the source body. It is not measured again, its refusal stands, and the other bodies of the file go on (`over_rounds` in `crates/catallax-import/src/tolerant/admission.rs`). A body that fails a round for another reason is still tried by the next, and an edge has one allowance for all the rounds of its body. What each edge counted is kept by the name of its `EDGE_CURVE` (`fit::Context::edge_spent`), on either outcome of a round, and the next round starts the edge with it. Where sewing replaced an edge by another, the retained edge starts with what both counted, and a round adds only its own part under the retained edge's name, so nothing is carried twice. So an edge that spends part of its allowance before a later, ordinary failure of its body has only the rest in the repair round.

On reopen there is one more boundary, before those rounds. `tolerant/opened.rs` first tries the whole file, then each body with its healed candidate, and gives what is still not taken to the native admission, which measures again with fresh allowances. A body whose own refusal is the cap's is left out there (`still_to_admit`), with its refusal, and its siblings go on. The classifiers of refusals that stop every body, `control_refusal` there and `native::control` in the fit, do not know the cap and are not widened: the check is of the body's own refusal.

The whole-file attempt is the first of those boundaries. Its refusal is one error for the file, so its admission also says which solid an edge's cap refused (`numerical::EdgeCap` in `crates/catallax-import/src/numerical.rs`). `checked_admit` stores the cap's refusal on the source body of that name and does not build, heal or measure that body again. The file's other bodies are then tried one by one as before.

Within a round an edge's allowance is kept by the edge's place in the body's edge table, and that table has one entry for each `EDGE_CURVE` of the file (`edge_indices` maps the name to the place). No repair splits an edge in two: a crossing cut shortens two existing edges, and the collapsed edge added at a pole has no u/v curve in the file, so the gap rule never reads it. So no repair gives part of an edge an allowance of its own.

OpenCASCADE and SMLib have no work cap to compare with. Both measure one face's copy of an edge at a time (`BRepCheck_Edge::InContext`, `SmEdgeuse::CalcMaxEdgeFaceGap`), which is the unit of one reading here too. The cap is Catallax's own, and the decisions set it per edge.

## Re-tightening

The decisions ask to "re-tighten tolerant edges between exact surfaces after each operation, as Parasolid's `PK_EDGE_optimise` and ACIS tweak do". Admission ends every numerical operation by building the body's model, so that is where it happens (`crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/retighten.rs`).

Admission gives every edge the tolerance it measured between the edge's 3D curve and its copy on each face. An edge whose 3D curve lies exactly on the surfaces of both its faces needs none. It is an edge at default precision in Jackson's sense, one 3D curve and no tube. The model stores it exact and records the two `Incident` facts an exact builder would have recorded, so `Model::check` passes it as it passes any exact edge.

Parasolid and ACIS intersect the two surfaces again and replace the edge's curve. Here the curve is kept and tested. A line or a circle lies on a plane, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere or a torus when a few polynomial identities hold between their defining numbers. Where those numbers are rationals, rational arithmetic decides the identities. They are the ones the certified STEP import uses (`check_on_surface` in `crates/catallax-import/src/build.rs`). An edge that fails one, or whose numbers are not all rational, keeps its measured tolerance. No decision is made and nothing is charged.

A spline has one identity: on a plane. Each of its points is a weighted mean of its control points, so a spline whose every control point is on a plane lies on it. A straight edge that a fit or an import wrote as a spline between two exact planes is re-tightened by it. A spline on any other surface keeps its tolerance: nothing is intersected.

A vertex of a numerical body is a rounded point, so it keeps its tolerance, and vertex >= edge allows that. The tolerance report is unchanged. It still states what admission measured for every edge, and export still reads it.

## Tolerant entities and the exact boolean

"Tolerant entities never reach the exact intersector." `catallax_boolean::boolean` is the one entry to the exact intersector for two bodies. It now refuses `boolean.tolerant-participant` when either body stores a tolerance on a vertex, an edge or a face (`Model::tolerant_entity`), before it makes any decision. An exact body is answered as before.

## Decisions with a tolerant participant

"Exact-exact decisions keep today's exact predicates. Any decision with a tolerant participant is 'distance against the sum of tolerances' through a filtered predicate: f64, then interval, then exact (the CGAL and Shewchuk pattern; Catallax already uses it in mesh and field)."

`crates/catallax-topology/src/numerical/predicate.rs` is that predicate. A decision is the sign of a polynomial of rational operands: a squared distance less a squared reach, where the reach is a size plus the participants' tolerances. The sign is read in three stages, and a stage is asked only where the one before could not tell.

1. Binary64, with a bound of its own rounding error kept beside every value (Higham's running error, "Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms", section 3.3). The sign is taken where the value is larger than its bound. This is the first stage of Shewchuk's predicates (1997), with the bound computed beside the value.
2. Outward-rounded binary64 intervals (`catallax_numbers::Interval`), the dynamic filter of Bronnimann, Burnikel and Pion (2001) that CGAL's filtered kernel uses.
3. Rational arithmetic.

The sign returned is the exact one, zero included. The stages change the cost and never the answer, so a decision between two exact entities that goes through them is still an exact decision. One unit is charged before each of the first two stages, and the third reserves each rational operation by the limb rule before it runs, as the decider does.

The decisions built on it: a point against a line at a reach, a segment against a line at a reach (two more signs say whether its nearest point is an end or the foot of the common perpendicular), the turn of two points about the origin, and the distances of the pair rule: two points, a point and a segment, and two segments, each against a reach.

OpenCASCADE decides the same question in binary64 alone: `IntTools_Context::ComputeVE` and `ComputeVF` compare a distance with `aTolV + aTolE + Max(theFuzz, Precision::Confusion())` (V7_8_0, `IntTools_Context.cxx`). SMLib's Boolean, `SmMerge`, takes one distance tolerance for the pair of bodies, from the sum or the larger of their tolerances, also in binary64 (`SmMerge.cpp`). The criterion here is OpenCASCADE's, per entity. The second and third stages are what the decisions add.

Only rational operands have a third stage. A curved edge or a curved face has no polynomial of this kind: the decisions rule the exact stage out for it ("the exact fallback is impractical on splines"). The hole below bounds such an entity by boxes, and where the boxes cannot tell it refuses.

The pair rule of the tolerance rules (T5) compares a distance with a sum of tolerances: an error where `t_a + t_b > d/2`, a warning where it is over `d/5`. It now goes through the stages as far as each pair allows (`pair_rule_check` in `tolerance/pairs.rs`).

- A pair of vertices and straight edges with rational coordinates has an exact distance: between two points, a point and a segment, or two segments (Ericson, "Real-Time Collision Detection", section 5.1.9, for the cases of two segments). Its two comparisons are signs of polynomials, read through the three stages. Every such pair the box tree returns is decided this way and no search is made for it.
- Any other pair has a curved edge or a face, and its distance is what the search in binary64 finds. The checker's evaluations state a bound of their own rounding, and the distance with the two entities' bounds to either side is its interval. An error is stated only where the whole interval is under `2 (t_a + t_b)`. Where the interval holds that value the pair refuses `numerical.tolerance-pair-unresolved`: there is no exact stage for a curved pair, and the result says so by name.

The search itself is as 3.0A built it. It finds a least distance by samples and polish, and what it finds is at least the true one.

## A hole in a body with tolerant entities

"The `features.numerical-continuation` refusal goes away: a hole after a numerical fillet works."

The exact hole subtracts a tool body with the Boolean, and tolerant entities never reach the exact intersector. So a hole in a body with tolerant entities has a construction of its own, `crates/catallax-features/src/hole/through_tolerant.rs`. It intersects nothing. It decides where the tool's cylinder is against every entity of the body, each at a reach of the tool's radius plus the entity's tolerance, and then writes the hole into the body's definition.

- Every vertex is farther from the axis than its reach, by the point predicate.
- Every straight edge is farther than its reach, by the segment predicate.
- Every other edge is cut into pieces, each enclosed in a box by the curve's own interval evaluation, until every box is beyond the reach. The test of a box is the squared distance over it in binary64 intervals. A piece whose box is no larger than the edge's tolerance and still not beyond the reach is within tolerance of the tool, and the hole refuses. An exact edge is cut down to rule T1's 1e-7 mm.
- A planar face is crossed where the axis passes inside its loops. The pieces of its edges are known to be clear of the axis, each in a convex box, so the winding of the pieces' end points round the axis is the winding of the loops themselves. The turns of the winding go through the predicate.
- Every other face is clear of the tool, by boxes over its parameter rectangle.

The crossings pair along the axis, into the material and out of it. Each crossing gets four vertices and four quarter circles as a new inner loop of its face, and each pair four lines and four quarter cylinders. Admission then measures the body, re-tightens the new circles and lines, and runs `Model::check`. The result has exact and tolerant entities side by side: on the owner's pocket with its rim rounded, the fillet's fitted edges keep their tolerances and the twelve new edges store none.

OpenCASCADE's `BRepFeat_MakeCylindricalHole::Perform` finds where the axis crosses the shape and with which orientation (`LocOpe_CurveShapeIntersector`), builds a cylinder and runs the Boolean. The first half is followed here. The Boolean is replaced by writing the rings down, which is possible because the tool is square to the faces it crosses, so every new edge is a circle or a line. SMLib has no hole feature.

What is built is a through hole of one radius, square to the planar faces it crosses, with a rational frame. Each of these refuses by name:

| Case | Refusal |
|---|---|
| a blind hole, a counterbore or a countersink | `hole.numerical-tool` |
| a tool whose point, direction or seam direction is not rational | `hole.numerical-frame` |
| a vertex or an edge within its tolerance of the tool, or a curved face the tool is not proven clear of | `hole.numerical-not-clear` |
| a planar face crossed at an angle | `hole.numerical-oblique-face` |
| crossings that do not pair into the material and out of it | `hole.numerical-crossings` |

A hole through a fitted face refuses `hole.numerical-not-clear`. It would need the intersection of the tool with a fitted surface, which this construction never makes.

The clearance of a curved face is tested over the parameter rectangle of its trims, not over the trimmed face. A tool that passes the face but meets the part of its surface that the trims cut away is refused. That is the safe side.

Every other operation that reads a body with tolerant entities still has no construction, and refuses `features.tolerant-input` with the operation's name (`Context::exact_inputs` in `crates/catallax-features/src/evaluate_numerical_fillet.rs`). The name `features.numerical-continuation` is gone from the code. A second fillet, a shell, a pattern or a Boolean after a numerical fillet is not part of 3.0B.
