# 3.0B the predictor

Roadmap item 3.0B, the predictor, of [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md), section 4.5 (Q13). The research behind it is [the predictor note](../research/cp2-grill/predictor-research.md). It covers step 1, the work meter, then the quick look, the tiers and the cap, and the frozen corpus.

## Step 1: the work meter

The decisions ask for two fixes before anything reads the meter.

1. Refunded speculative work has its own counter, `OperationWork::refunded`. A refused optional attempt gives its charges back to the allowance, and what it charged stays on the record.
2. The arithmetic the meter did not see is counted, in `OperationWork::uncharged`.

The second fix was first built as a charge against the allowance for the STEP writer's curve fit. It changed answers: one frozen request and six STEP regression requests that answer today refused `numbers.operation-budget`. The owner ruled on 21 November 2026 that the arithmetic is counted in its own counter and charged to nothing. A written curve has no cheaper construction for a cap to fall back to, which is step 5 of the decisions: no cap where no fitted path exists.

Counting the fit alone did not complete the meter. Measured over 53 exports, the fit is 37% of export time. The rest is ball arithmetic in the pcurve fit, sums in the span errors, decimal rounding, and on one part a boundary table in `catallax-shapes`, and builds have the same gap. The owner's second ruling of the same day: make the meter complete, count every ball and rational operation, charge nothing.

### What is counted

`Ball` and `Rational` count themselves as they run, so no caller can leave an operation out.

| Operation | Count |
|---|---|
| Ball sum, difference | 4L |
| Ball product, square, reciprocal, square root | 8L^2 |
| Ball quotient | its reciprocal and its product |
| Rational sum, difference | the two operands' limbs added |
| Rational product, quotient | the two operands' limbs multiplied |
| Rational power | one product fewer than the exponent, each the power by the base |
| Decimal conversion | the value's limbs squared |

L is ceil(p / 64) for a working precision of p bits. A rational's limbs are its numerator's plus its denominator's, at 64 bits a limb. Every operation counts one unit more.

These are the decider's own prices (`Work::ball` and `Work::exact` in `crates/catallax-numbers/src/decider.rs`, under "Logical arithmetic costs"), so a unit of `uncharged` is a unit of `used`. They are the limb operations of schoolbook addition and multiplication (Knuth, The Art of Computer Programming, volume 2, section 4.3.1, algorithms A and M). A decimal conversion converts and divides, each quadratic in the value's limbs (the same section, algorithm D, and section 4.4). The one unit more is the unit every charge of the decider pays for its deadline check.

`used + refunded + uncharged` is a request's arithmetic counted once.

### What is not counted, and why

- Arithmetic inside a decision. A decision charges its arithmetic to `used` as it goes, and that charge is its price. The count is closed while a decision's account is open.
- Arithmetic inside an operation that is counted whole. A ball operation and a decimal conversion are one operation each.
- The fill of a memo that lives on a shared value and is not charged: a value's own rational and coarse forms (`real.rs`), a patch's normal polynomials, a curve's equations in the store, a trace's jet program, the saddle net's turn bounds, the fit's fixed weights and the Groebner basis of an intersection (`catallax-intersect/src/solve.rs`). Whichever thread or request reads the value first fills the memo, once, so a count of the fill would differ with the thread count and with what ran before. The decider prices such a memo at nothing ("Construction-time caches spend no decision budget"). The fill is wrapped in `uncounted_memo`.
- Comparisons, negations, shifts by a power of two and conversions between the types. The decider has no price for them.
- `Integer` arithmetic done directly. The decider prices rationals and balls.

### The reduction of a rational

A rational is kept in lowest terms, so each sum and product ends in a greatest common divisor. The prices above are those of the sum or the product alone. Measured on the corpus, the divisor was most of the time of the slowest rows: 63% of the instructions of one loft cut and 55% of one fitted fillet with its export. `num-integer`'s algorithm for a long integer is Stein's binary one, about 32 passes over both operands for each limb against the one pass a limb of a schoolbook product takes, and it ran in full when one operand was 1.

`crates/catallax-numbers/src/gcd.rs` brings the reduction near its price. A unit operand costs nothing. Operands of at most 128 bits use the binary algorithm on machine words (Knuth, volume 2, section 4.5.2, Algorithm B). Longer ones use Lehmer's algorithm (the same section, Algorithm L). Two rationals whose parts fit a machine word are added and multiplied by Knuth's method for fractions (section 4.5.1), which takes every divisor of single words and none of the double-word cross products. A divisor is unique, so every value, every charge and every count is what it was. The loft cut that ran for 62 seconds runs for 12, and on the 97 tuning rows wall time per million counted units went from an 18.6x band to 7.9x.

A count of the Groebner fill at every use, found or filled, was built first and removed. A basis is found far more often than it is filled, so it counted 4,400 million units on that cut. With the reduction near its price the fill is an ordinary memo again.

Two cases need a rule so that nothing is counted twice.

- `Decider::reserve_exact_arithmetic` charges `used` for a rational operation its caller runs next, outside the decision. The operation counts itself, so a reservation that succeeds takes the same amount off the count. A caller whose arithmetic never counts itself reserves with `reserve_uncounted_arithmetic`, which charges the same and takes nothing off: an operation of a `Real`, which runs later inside the value's memo, a comparison of two rationals, a shift, a floor, a ceiling or a larger-of, and integer arithmetic.
- `Decider::charge_work` is a caller's own tally of its loop. It pays for no operation of the number types. Arithmetic its caller runs is counted, so a caller that charges its ball arithmetic by hand shows that arithmetic in both counters.

### The same at any thread count

The count is that of the request's own thread.

- A helper thread's count reaches the request only with a record the request adopts (`Charges::adopt`), as that record's charges reach `used`.
- A checked pi enclosure is reused within a request. A helper that runs the series keeps its count with the proof, and a replay counts it where it charges the series: when the request's cache does not hold the enclosure.
- An item of a numeric batch (`OperationTask`) reports its count as it reports its refused attempts, admitted or discarded.
- A reserved allowance run inside a request has a counter of its own.
- A memo whose fill is charged is already left alone by helpers (`shared_caches`), so the request's thread fills it and counts it as one thread does.

### What other kernels do

OpenCASCADE and SMLib have no deterministic work meter to compare with. The practice this follows is the deterministic limit of a solver: Z3's `rlimit` counts basic operations and Gurobi's `WorkLimit` stops at the same point on the same hardware, as the predictor research records. The price list is Catallax's decider's own.

## Steps 2 to 7: the quick look, the tiers and the cap

An exact attempt that has a fitted construction behind it runs under a work cap. A quick look at the attempt's inputs picks one of two caps. This is design (e) of the research note: a prediction sets the cap and a deterministic limit enforces it. A wrong tier costs at most the cap. It never changes what is accepted, because the construction that takes over is the one that already follows any refusal of the attempt, with its own admission.

### The cap

`catallax_numbers::capped_attempt` is `refund_on_refusal` with a limit of its own. Inside it, `cap_attempt(units)` sets the cap, counted from the attempt's start. A charge past the cap stops the attempt as a spent allowance stops a request, but only the attempt. Its charges go back to the allowance and into `OperationWork::refunded`, the request is not exhausted, and the caller gets the refusal `numbers.attempt-cap` to hand to the fitted construction. An attempt that finishes under its cap keeps every charge and its value, so its request costs what it did before. A cap at or above what the request has left changes nothing.

The cap is a number of work units, so an attempt stops at the same charge on every machine and at every thread count. An item of a numeric batch started inside the attempt is given what the cap leaves, not what the request has.

`cap_attempt_when_reached(first, decide)` puts the choice of the cap off. The attempt may charge `first` units. The first charge that would pass them asks `decide`, once, and the attempt goes on under the cap it returns. An attempt that ends before `first` never asks. The charge that asks is the same at every thread count: a recorded computation is adopted only where all of it fits under `first` and is run charge by charge otherwise, and a cap that was put off is decided before an allowance is divided among items or a capped attempt begins inside.

### The quick look

`crates/catallax-features/src/predictor.rs`. It reads three things of each pair of surfaces the attempt will bring together.

- D, the product of the two implicit degrees. A plane has degree 1, a quadric 2, a torus 4, and a tensor-product patch of degree (p, q) has 2pq, so a bicubic has 18 (Sederberg, Anderson and Goldman 1984).
- L, the largest limb count among the rationals that define the two surfaces, numerator and denominator added, at 64 bits a limb. A number that is not a rational counts as a small one, because it is decided through enclosures at a working precision.
- Whether the pair is proven apart. Such a pair adds nothing.

The score of a pair is D x L^2 and the score of an operation is that of its costliest pair. The look reads no normal angle. The decisions allow one only to demote spline pairs, and D already places those. It keeps no memo between steps and has no learned part.

The look runs no arithmetic the number types count. Its own work is one unit for everything it reads: each number and each kind of a surface, and each step through the topology that finds the pairs, which is one for a selected edge, one for each use of it and one for each pair. The faces of an edge are found through the edge's own uses, so the look costs by the selection and not by the size of the body. All of it is counted in `OperationWork::uncharged` and charged to nothing, under the same ruling as the rest of step 1. CI holds that accepted work never rises on the released edits, and a charge here would raise it.

### When the look is taken

An attempt that cannot reach the smaller cap finishes the same under either, so its tier does not matter. The look is therefore taken when the attempt has charged the high tier's 2,000,000 units, through `cap_attempt_when_reached`, and not before. A fillet that ends under that is never looked at and pays nothing. One that gets there is read once, and the look reads at most `LOOK_READS`, 20,000, a hundredth of what the attempt has charged: "It costs at most 1% of the operation." A surface with more numbers than are left is not read to its end and is not taken for a low-complexity one, and a look that runs out among the edges, uses or pairs stops there and takes the high tier.

The look was first taken before the attempt began. It read 22 values of a plane and a sphere, which is 2.1% of a fillet whose whole work is 1,035 units, and six small fillets of the corpus were over the line.

### The tiers

| Tier | Score | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Low | D x L^2 <= 32 | 20,000,000, the work target of one build |
| High | above | 2,000,000 |

The two caps are the decisions' numbers. The boundary is D = 8 on small rationals, L = 2. D = 8 is the research note's line for the pairs of a plane, a quadric and a torus, after ESOLID's limit of algebraic degree 4 for exact boundary evaluation (Keyser and others 2004). L = 2 is one limb for the numerator and one for the denominator, which is the least a rational has.

### Where it runs

Only where a fitted construction exists. Today that is one place, a fillet built through `evaluate_incremental_outcome`, where the rolling ball follows a refusal of the exact builders. The exact builders set the cap once they have resolved their selection, so the selection is resolved once and the cap covers the attempt from its start. The pairs are the two faces of each selected edge. They share that edge, so none is ever proven apart.

A boolean has no fitted construction, so step 5 gives it no cap and the look is not run for it. The same holds for a fillet built through `evaluate`, which has no rolling ball behind it. `crates/catallax-measure/src/entity/gap.rs` also returns the work of a refused pass, but what follows it there is the answer the earlier search gave, not a fitted construction, so it is left as it was.

### What other kernels do

No kernel read for the research note predicts the cost of a whole operation before running it. OpenCASCADE and SMLib run one construction and report its failure. Parasolid and ACIS fall back to a tolerant edge after an exact intersection fails. The limit follows solver practice: Z3's `rlimit`, Gurobi's `WorkLimit`, and the per-level iteration limit of QSopt_ex.

## The frozen corpus

`tools/predictor/corpus.json`, run by `crates/catallax-cli/examples/predictor_corpus.rs`. Each row is built through the application's ordinary build and then exported.

| Group | Rows | From |
|---|---:|---|
| checkpoint-1 | 20 | `parts/`, `parts/P0`, `parts/holes`, `tools/checkpoint/phase2` |
| fillets and the box fillet | 50 | `parts/fillets/cases.jsonl` |
| phase-5 | 27 | the 13 M8 cuts, ten owner rim fillets, the M10 top cut, three M11 loft fillets |
| held-out | 20 | ABC models |

The held-out rule was fixed before any threshold was run. From `docs/research/step-census/results.csv` it takes the models whose import is complete and certified, leaves out the ten retained controls, sorts by model id and keeps the first 20. Each row imports the model and rounds every edge at 1/10 mm. The sources are private, so those rows need `--sources` and `--selection`.

`predictor_corpus run` writes one line for each row, thread count and pass. `predictor_corpus check` first tests coverage: every row of the corpus at 1, 2, 4 and 24 threads on each of two machines, a machine being a run's label. A missing row, thread count or machine fails the check, and so does a row missing from the base run. It then tests the done-when lines on those lines: the same path and work in every line of a row, the look at most 1% of its operation's counted work where one was taken, refunded work within its cap, and wall time per million counted units within a 10x band over measurements of 10 ms or more, read at one thread and on each machine by itself, since two machines' clocks are not one scale. With `--base` it also tests that every row exact within the 20M target on the base revision is still exact, with the same charged work and the same exported bytes.

## The same work on two machines

The decisions ask that path choice and work are identical "at 1, 2, 4 and 24 threads and on two machines". On main the second half did not hold where a fillet is built by the rolling ball. Between the work PC (x86_64 Linux) and a Mac (arm64 macOS), the eight rim fillets of the corpus that build fitted and one that both constructions refuse differed in charged work by up to 0.4% and in exported bytes. Path choice, the exact attempt's work and the look were the same on both.

The cause was the binary64 code of the fitted fillet: 31 calls of `f64`'s own `sin`, `cos`, `sin_cos`, `atan2`, `acos` and `hypot`, which go to the platform's math library and differ in the last bit from one machine to the next. They now go through the `libm` crate, the musl library's routines in Rust, as `general_sweep.rs` already did. The files are `fillet_native.rs`, `numerical_embedding.rs` with its `planar.rs`, `guides.rs` and `rings.rs`, `fillet_numerical_vertex_boundaries.rs` and `deform.rs` in `catallax-features`, and `spline_fillet/numerical_analytic.rs` in `catallax-shapes`.

With it, all 117 rows have the same status, charged work, refunded work, count, export work, exported bytes, path choices and per-operation work on both machines. On the work PC it changes 12 rows against main: the nine above and three held-out rows whose fillet both constructions refuse. No status changes and no exact row changes.

The pair rule of the tolerance rules adds to the same rows. It now reads the rounding of each entity of a measured pair and decides a straight pair exactly (see the one-model note), which costs each fitted rim fillet between 2,056 and 2,131 units and the smooth loft fillet 267. That makes 13 rows whose charged work differs from main, by at most 0.79%, up or down. None of them is exact.

One call of the platform's library is left in a path the corpus runs: a fourth root in an estimate of the exact STEP export (`catallax-step/src/export/mitre.rs`). No row differs between the machines through it, and changing it could move exact work, so it is left alone.

## The measured lines

The corpus was run on the milestone's code on the work PC (24 threads, x86_64 Linux): all 117 rows, three passes at one thread, then 2, 4 and 24 threads, 702 lines. The same 117 rows were run on a second machine (8 cores, arm64 macOS) at 1, 2, 4 and 24 threads, one pass each, 468 lines. The same runner without the predictor was run on main `e6fabf911`, the revision the milestone is based on. Only the checker's band line and these notes changed after the runs.

| Line of the decisions | Result |
|---|---|
| path choice and work identical at 1, 2, 4 and 24 threads | holds: no row differs between its ten lines, six on the work PC and four on the Mac |
| and on two machines | holds: no row of the 117 differs between the two machines, after the change above |
| the quick look costs at most 1% of each operation's work | holds: 102 attempts, 3 looked at, the largest share 0.0006% (109 read against 17,886,359 units, the steps through the topology included) |
| everything that builds exact under 20M today still does | holds: 80 rows are exact within 20M on main, and each is exact at the head with the same charged work, the same export work and the same exported bytes |
| refunded work stays within its tier cap | holds: of the 17 attempts that were refused or went to the rolling ball, the largest gave back 592,353 units, under the high tier's 2,000,000 |
| wall time per million work units within a 10x band | at the limit, and accepted there by the owner: 9.6x, 9.8x and 10.6x in three earlier runs on the work PC, then 9.8x on the work PC and 9.6x on the Mac in the runs of this table |
| thresholds unchanged on the held-out set | holds: no threshold was changed after the tuning rows were run |

The fourth line holds for more than it asks: 104 of the 117 rows have the same status, charged work, export work and exported bytes on main and at the head, and the other 13 are the rows of the section above. The builds of the corpus take 115 seconds on main and 72 to 89 at the head, by how busy the machine is. The difference from main is the reduction of rationals.

### The band

On the work PC the two ends are the same two measurements in every run: the build of the lofted duct, 319 million counted units in about 1.95 seconds (6.1 ms per million), and the STEP export of the smooth loft fillet, 8.3 million units in 0.49 to 0.58 seconds (58.7 to 64.8). The slow end moves by 15% between passes, which carries the band across the line. On the 97 tuning rows of a quieter day it read 7.9x. In the runs of the table the work PC was busier and both ends were slower by a quarter (7.6 and 74.7), which leaves the band where it was. On the Mac the ends are the build of a held-out model (6.0) and the build of the block with a sphere (57.2).

It sits there because the prices are the decider's. A ball product at one limb is priced 8 units and takes about 50 ns. A product of two one-limb rationals is priced 4 units and takes about 250 ns, most of it in allocating the result and reducing it. The fast end is all ball arithmetic and the slow end all small rationals. A profile of the slow row has the reduction at 10% of its instructions, allocation at 22% and big-integer shifts and products at 12%, and no uncounted operation left to find.

A rational kept inline while its parts fit a machine word would take the allocation out and give the line margin. It changes the number type under every exact computation, so it is not in this milestone. The owner accepted the line at the limit for 3.0B on 21 Nov 2026, on the readings 9.6x, 9.8x and 10.6x. The inline rational is a later, separate change.

### The tiers

The three attempts that were looked at are the oblique partial fillet (5.5 million units) and the two ruled loft fillets (10.3 and 14.5 million). All three read as the low tier and finish exact under its cap. No attempt of the corpus reads as the high tier and none reaches a cap: the fillets on fitted faces refuse within 592,353 units, before the look is taken. So the corpus shows that the caps cost nothing where the exact builders finish. It has no row that the 2,000,000 cap stops.

### The held-out rows

Of the 20, 15 build exact on the exact route, 2 are refused at import (`import.multisolid-separation`) and 3 fillets are refused by both constructions. Main gives the same.

### Rows that are not in the corpus

Their requests live in the private proof-inputs repository or are built by test code: the M10-004 to 006 top cuts and the 2.3 sheet chain, the 3.3 relation and four-contact queries (which build nothing), the multi-patch loft fillet with its radius edit, the imported-part fillet, and the sample part's 12 edges.

- `crates/catallax-numbers/tests/run_ahead.rs`, `recorded_computations_charge_what_cold_ones_do`: a recorded computation's count is the same adopted or run cold, at every allowance.
- `crates/catallax-step/src/fit/tests.rs`: the fit's arithmetic is counted without a call of its own.
- `crates/catallax-numbers/src/operation.rs`: a cap stops the attempt and not the request, an attempt under its cap keeps its charges, a cap above what is left changes nothing, a cap binds only the attempt it is set in, an item inside a capped attempt shares what the cap leaves, and an attempt that unwinds leaves the request its limit. For a cap that is put off: it is asked at the first charge past the first cap and at no other, a cap asked inside a plain attempt stays with the capped one, one never asked ends with its attempt, it is asked before an allowance is divided or another cap is set, and an attempt that unwinds takes it along.
- `crates/catallax-features/src/predictor.rs`: the tier by degree and limbs, a pair proven apart, what is read of a surface, and a surface with more numbers than may be read.
- `crates/catallax-features/tests/predictor.rs`: the small controls. One block and one fillet, with the block's position in short or in long rationals, so its edges read as the low or the high tier. The fillet ends far under the smaller cap, so it is not looked at: it keeps its body, its charged work, its count and its check. Built with no fitted construction, the same recipe sets no cap. A selection too long to read stops the look at its bound, with every step counted, and the look takes the high tier.
