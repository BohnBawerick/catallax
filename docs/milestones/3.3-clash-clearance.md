# 3.3 clash, clearance and mass

Roadmap 3.3 (old M17): "Clash, clear by a stated gap, or unresolved, for every pair." Why here: "Exact overlap already works. Distant and nested pairs are left." Done when: "Every pair in a test assembly gets one of the three answers."

The B-rep query is `catallax_app::queries::body_relation`. It answers `Clash` with a proven positive overlap volume, `Clear` with a proven positive gap, or refuses with a typed reason, which is the unresolved answer. The field's `catallax_field::assembly::relation` stays a test-time yardstick, decision Q16 of the [Checkpoint 2 decisions](../checkpoints/checkpoint-2-decisions.md). [M17-RELATION-MASS.md](../M17-RELATION-MASS.md) has the earlier contract and the mass queries.

The milestone lands in steps, one pull request each:

1. Distant pairs.
2. Nested and overlapping pairs where the Boolean refuses.
3. A certified answer for strata pairs with no closed form that hold the minimum, so their stated gap meets the requested accuracy.
4. The test assembly, with the field relation as yardstick.

Pairs with a tolerant participant, a body carrying a fitted-geometry mark, keep refusing `app.relation-construction-bound` until roadmap 3.0B gives tolerant entities their predicates.

## Step 1: distant pairs

### What refused before

A probe placed copies of P1 to P4 and of a spline loft 1 mm apart, 500 mm apart and turned a quarter turn, and paired block, sphere, cylinder, cone and torus solids at several poses. Of 115 pairs, 64 refused. Three causes covered almost all of them:

- `body_relation` took the minimum over every face pair, each face pair on its own. A face pair far from the minimum still refused `measure.distance-uncovered` when two of its edges had no closed form, for example two circles with non-parallel normals. Every distant or near copy of P1 to P4 refused this way, and the P4 copies spent 43M to 63M work first.
- A block turned by 30 degrees has parallel faces on planes with no rational frame. The planar witness search refused `measure.irrational-plane`.
- Two P3 copies 1 mm apart refused in the overlap Boolean, `intersect.traced-pair-required`, because their torus fillets form a non-coaxial torus pair. The Boolean ran even on solids whose boxes are far apart, at 20M to 43M work for P3 and P4.

### How OpenCASCADE and SMLib do it

OpenCASCADE's `BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape` splits both shapes into vertices, edges and faces, computes a box for each, keeps the pairs whose boxes are nearer than the best distance found, sorts them by box distance and evaluates them in that order with `Extrema`. Its `Extrema_ExtElC::PlanarLineCircleExtrema` answers a line in, or parallel to, a circle's plane by working in that plane; a general line and circle go to a trigonometric equation of degree four, and two circles have a closed form only when they are coplanar. SMLib's `SmApiBrepRelationship` (in `SM_API/src/SmApiQueries.cpp`) first takes the minimum distance between the two boundaries, then classifies one closest point against the other solid to tell separate pairs from containment; step 2 follows it.

Step 1 follows `BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape` for the order and the pruning, and keeps Catallax's exact closed forms for each pair, so a value is exact where the closed form is.

### The change

`catallax_measure::solid_gap` takes the minimum distance between the closed boundaries of two solids that do not cross:

- Each face's strata (the face, its edges, its vertices) and its proven box are computed once. Face pairs are visited in order of their boxes' separation. Once the best distance is no greater than a pair's box separation, that pair and every later one are skipped.
- The strata search of a visited pair skips any strata pair that cannot come below the best distance of the whole search, not only of its own face pair.
- A line parallel to a circle's plane now has a closed form: the stationary pairs of the line's projection into the plane, lifted back along the normal. This is OpenCASCADE's planar line-circle case.
- Two parallel planar faces whose planes have no rational frame no longer refuse. The pairs at the plane gap form a closed region of the overlap of the two projected faces. A boundary point of that region lies on an edge or vertex of one face, where the same minimum is stationary again, and the search visits those strata.
- A strata pair with no closed form whose boxes are nearer than the final minimum is settled face pair by face pair. `catallax_shapes::distance::face_distance_below` runs the existing certified interval search over the two closed faces with the minimum as its ceiling. It discards a cell pair as soon as its covers are that far apart. It either proves the faces no nearer than the minimum or finds a nearer admitted witness. All open face pairs share one cell budget, the certified search's default for one face pair.
- With a witness found, settling only tightens a proven bound, so it runs inside an `OperationAllowance` of as much work again as the closed-form search spent. Planar faces bounded by spline edges make every cell an exact trim test against a spline; without this cap one such pair of the turned loft spent the whole 100M allowance. Without a witness, settling is the only route to an answer and runs within the request's allowance.
- What settling cannot settle widens the stated gap down to the lower bound it proved. The answer then carries the `measure.accuracy-not-reached` mark, the bounded answer kind. The query refuses `measure.distance-uncovered` only without any witness pair.

`body_relation` now:

- proves zero overlap from the two solids' proven boxes when they are strictly apart, without the Boolean;
- calls `solid_gap` for the gap;
- answers `Clear` for any proven positive gap, carrying the mark when the enclosure is wider than the requested accuracy. `app.relation-gap` now means a gap not proven positive.

The [interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-21-solid-gap.md) lists the API changes.

## Step 2: nested and overlapping pairs where the Boolean refuses

### What refused before

After step 1, a pair whose proven boxes meet still went through the overlap Boolean, and a Boolean refusal was the answer. A second probe placed P2, P3 and loft copies overlapping by half and by a quarter of their width and turned 30 degrees in place, a sphere at the loft's box centre, a pin turned 30 degrees across and beside a cone's wall, and a small torus turned 30 degrees inside and across the tube of a large one. The Boolean refused all of them, for four reasons:

- cone and cylinder axes that are neither perpendicular nor parallel (`intersect.trace-native-window`, `intersect.trace-native-cover`);
- non-coaxial torus pairs (`intersect.traced-pair-required`);
- spline pairs with irrational coefficients (`intersect.spline-pair-irrational`, `shapes.parametric-query`);
- loft intersections whose faces the volume integrator does not support (`measure.unsupported-surface`).

### How OpenCASCADE and SMLib do it

OpenCASCADE's `BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape::SolidTreatment` classifies each vertex of one shape against the other solid with `BRepClass3d_SolidClassifier`. A vertex inside is an inner solution at distance 0. SMLib's `SmApiBrepRelationship` takes the minimum distance between the two boundaries first. When it is positive, it classifies the two closest points to tell separate solids from one inside the other. When it is zero, it samples vertices, one point inside each face and one point on each edge piece between crossings of the other boundary; any sample strictly inside the other solid makes the pair interpenetrating, otherwise touching. Neither returns an overlap volume. SMLib's own notes say its sampling may report an overlap that no sample lands in as touching.

Step 2 follows SMLib's order and OpenCASCADE's vertex test, with every decision proven. It adds what Catallax's clash answer needs and neither kernel gives, a proven positive lower bound for the overlap volume.

### The change

When the overlap Boolean refuses, or leaves the overlap enclosure unresolved, `body_relation` hands the pair to an arrangement search (`catallax_app::queries::arrangement`):

1. Boundary samples. Each shell's vertices, then one point strictly inside each face (`catallax_boolean::face_point`), are classified exactly against the other solid. A sample outside the other solid's proven box needs no ray. A solid bounded by one whole sphere or torus is classified by the sign of its equation. A shell with samples strictly inside and strictly outside the other solid crosses its boundary.
2. Grid points. When every shell has a sample outside the other solid and none inside, no solid lies wholly inside the other, so no solid's volume can be the overlap. Points of a dyadic grid over the two proven boxes' overlap are classified, coarsest level first, at most 73 points over the three levels of step 1's parallel planar witness search. A point inside both solids proves a clash.
3. Boundaries apart. Without a crossing, every face pair whose proven boxes, or rational chart control boxes, meet goes to the certified interval search (`face_distance_below`, with the search's default width as ceiling and all pairs sharing its default cell count, as `solid_gap` settles its open pairs). A positive proven lower bound sets the pair apart. With the boundaries apart, each shell lies wholly inside or wholly outside the other solid. Every shell outside gives a clear pair. Its gap comes from `solid_gap`, whose noncrossing precondition now holds, and is at least the least lower bound this proof found. Every shell of one solid inside the other, with none of the other's inside it, gives a clash whose overlap is the inner solid's volume.
4. A clash from a sample. A boundary point strictly inside the other solid, or a grid point inside both, proves a positive overlap. Its lower bound is a ball inside both solids. Take p on the boundary of X strictly inside Y, with a proven lower bound r of its distance to Y's boundary, so the ball of radius r about p lies in Y. A step of r/2 along X's inward normal at p, or along the mean of the normals at an edge or vertex, gives q. Once q is classified inside X with a proven lower bound rho of its distance to X's boundary, the ball of radius min(rho, r/2) about q lies in both solids. The overlap is then at least 4/3 pi min(rho, r/2)^3, more than 4 min(rho, r/2)^3. A grid point grows its ball the same way. The two proven boxes' overlap bounds the overlap from above. The answer carries `measure.accuracy-not-reached`, the bounded kind.
5. Otherwise the Boolean's refusal stands, with what the search proved added to its detail.

A ball needs a positive radius, not a distance to the search's default width. Each face's lower bound is therefore the distance to its whole carrier, from the closed forms of OpenCASCADE's `Extrema_ExtPElS` for a point and a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus, evaluated in ball arithmetic. A rational spline face takes the least bound over its charts' patches, from a best-first subdivision of each patch with `BSplineSurface::evaluate_box` enclosures. Faces are visited in order of their boxes' distance from the point and skipped once that distance reaches the bound found, as `solid_gap` prunes face pairs.

The [interface note](../interface-changes/2026-11-21-relation-arrangement.md) lists the API changes.

## Step 3: a certified answer where no closed form holds the minimum

### What stayed open

After step 1, a strata pair with no closed form whose boxes are nearer than the minimum went to the certified interval search over its two faces (`face_distance_below`). That search bounds a pair of parameter cells by the separation of their covers. Its lower bound gains on the minimum only as fast as the cells shrink, so near the minimum it cannot reach the default accuracy, 1e-12 relative, within its 4096 cells. The stated gap then kept the strata boxes' separation as its lower end. In step 1's probe, 16 clear answers carried the `measure.accuracy-not-reached` mark this way, and P2 beside P4 stated [5, 8] mm.

The strata pairs without a closed form are a line in general position against a circle, two circles with non-parallel normals, and a line, circle or cone against a cone. A cylinder face is its axis offset by the radius and a torus face is its core circle offset by the tube radius, so their pairs are line and circle pairs.

### How OpenCASCADE and SMLib do it

OpenCASCADE has no closed form for these pairs either. `Extrema_ExtElC` answers two circles only when they are coplanar. Other circle pairs go to `Extrema_GenExtCC`, which sweeps both parameters with `math_GlobOptMin` (Evtushenko's method with an estimated Lipschitz constant) and refines the best points with `math_NewtonMinimum`. A line and a circle in general position give a degree-four equation in the half-angle, which `math_TrigonometricFunctionRoots` solves. `Extrema_ExtElCS` leaves lines and circles against cones and tori unimplemented. `Extrema_GenExtCS` handles them: it seeds `math_PSO` along the curve, projects each point onto the quadric and refines with `math_FunctionSetRoot`. Two cones, or a cone and a torus, go to `Extrema_GenExtSS`, which takes the best pair of two 20 x 20 grids and runs BFGS from it. `BRepExtrema_DistanceSS` then keeps a solution only when `BRepClass_FaceClassifier` puts it inside both faces, and leaves minima on a face's boundary to the edge and vertex pairs.

SMLib's `SmGlobalSolver` subdivides Bezier spans, drops a branch whose boxes are farther apart than the best answer less a tolerance, and runs Newton at the leaves.

Neither proves a solution, or that no nearer one exists. Step 3 follows SMLib's subdivision and pruning, and OpenCASCADE's split into strata pairs with a trim test for each solution. It proves each step.

### The change

`solid_gap` gains a pass over the open strata pairs of every face pair the interval search left unsettled (`catallax-measure/src/entity/gap/stationary.rs`):

1. Each core is parametrized. A line uses arc length, a circle the rational half-angle parameter of each quarter turn, and a cone that parameter and its height. The strata's proven boxes bound the linear parameters.
2. Ball arithmetic encloses the stationary equations over a box of parameters: half the gradient of the squared core distance. The cone's turning equation is divided by its height, so the apex is not a singular point of the system.
3. Boxes are visited nearest first. A box is dropped when its carrier points cannot lie in the strata's boxes, or when no offset pair from it can come below the best distance found. It is also dropped when one equation keeps its sign on it, the monotonicity test of interval global optimization (Hansen and Walster, Global Optimization Using Interval Analysis, 2004, chapter 12).
4. The Krawczyk operator (Krawczyk 1969) contracts each remaining box, and drops it when the image misses the box. An image inside the box's interior proves exactly one solution there (Moore 1977). When the operator halves a box without that proof, it is tried again on a box of the same size centred on the smaller one. This catches a solution on a box's face, as Rump's epsilon-inflation does (Rump 1983).
5. The same operator refines each proven solution until a step no longer halves the box, or the box is as narrow as the working precision resolves. The mean value form encloses the solution's offset distance. Exact carrier points at the refined box's centre are tested on both trims; when both trims admit them, their distance is the upper bound.
6. A trim may reject the centre and still admit the solution, when the trim's boundary crosses the refined box. A point of that boundary then lies within the box's diagonal of the solution, on a lower stratum the search also visits. So the solution is dropped and its diagonals are subtracted from the final lower end. Each drop reaches a lower stratum, so the sum of the dropped diagonals bounds the loss.

The pass runs only where the old path left a face pair unsettled, so every answer that was within accuracy keeps its value and its work. It runs as an attempt whose refusal returns its work (`refund_on_refusal`). If it runs out of allowance, the answer is the one step 1 gave, and the refused work is recorded in `OperationWork::refunded`. One budget of 4096 boxes, the certified search's default for one face pair, serves every open strata pair, nearest first. Each stratum's parametrized core is built once per pass.

Ball arithmetic in the pass is charged at the decider's rates: 4L units a sum, 8L^2 a product, quotient or root, for L limbs of 64 bits. Checkpoint 2 decision Q13 asks the same of direct arithmetic in `fit.rs`. The search runs at 64 bits. A solution moves to 128 or 256 bits, the precisions `enclose` uses, only when its value misses the requested accuracy.

What stays open: strata whose curves have no exact line or circle form (spline edges), a solution on a cone's apex, and cores that meet where the carriers can share a normal. Those keep the lower bound proved for them, and the mark when it misses the accuracy.
