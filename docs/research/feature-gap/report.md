# SolidDraft feature creation and recognition gap

20 November 2026 · SolidDraft main `69628cc8ab8a3d9aaae4ef7a140b4f02f4aba6d2` · Quiddity 0.5.4 `9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051`

SolidDraft can create representative geometry for **34 of Quiddity’s 56 published feature families**. Another **20 are partly covered**. The two families without a reliable creation path in the pinned app are **modeled threads and round-wire helical springs**. Sketch and cut already cover most slots, notches, channels, steps and passages. The largest authoring gaps are common combinations where users need one editable definition for a shape they can sometimes assemble from several features today.

The owner placed A–K in [Later planning](../../../ROADMAP.md#later-planning-soliddraft-feature-gaps). The recommendations below record the original audit.

- **Original build-now recommendation.** Give Hole editable stages and callouts. Deliver the roadmap’s Solid Slot through the existing Cut route and expand it into a small profile-cut workflow. Add a two-direction feature grid. Continue the planned edge-finishing and post-fillet continuation work in its assigned lane.
- **First fixtures.** Start with the four C5 contexts already named in the roadmap, then create a multi-stage hole, two round-floor slot profiles, a two-direction pocket grid and concave/convex rounds. Quiddity’s prior P1 cross-check reported the bore, boss, hole arrangement and slot, plus two plates. The P1 run is evidence from an earlier inspection. This report did not rerun it.
- **Later.** Use the roadmap’s helix work for both printable threads and springs. Place semantic thread intent with Hole/PMI as soon as the shared data is ready. Sequence groove, rib, shell and surfacing work by the user jobs below. Scope sheet metal around a deliverable flat blank and bend table. A dedicated gear generator merits a user demand signal because simple authored radial repeats can already exercise that recognizer.

### What the family count means

The five published rows missing from the README are `internal_bevels`, `local_steps`, `polygonal_oblique_through_steps`, `prismatic_through_cuts`, and `section_recess_patterns`. Publication folds three working variants into existing families: `oriented_slots` into `slots`, `oriented_chamfers` into `chamfers`, and `oriented_slot_patterns` into `slot_patterns`. The matrix includes those variants under their public names. Through, blind, counterbored, spotfaced and countersunk hole measures and nested sheet bends/flanges are variants or child records. Section recesses and some patterns are derived from constituent features. Treat these as published recognition coverage, even where several records share evidence. [Recognition 6, merged families](https://github.com/pzfreo/quiddity/blob/9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051/docs/recognition-6.md#L63-L83), [section-recess projection](https://github.com/pzfreo/quiddity/blob/9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051/src/features/section_recess_family.rs#L1-L15), [sheet outputs](https://github.com/pzfreo/quiddity/blob/9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051/src/features/sheet_metal.rs#L71-L188).

For completeness, the document also publishes these refusal-only namespaces: `modelled_thread_refusals`, `pad_refusals`, `pocket_refusals`, `prismatic_pocket_refusals`, `section_passage_refusals`, `section_recess_source_refusals`, `oriented_slot_refusals`, `reconciliation_refusals`, `polygonal_boss_refusals`, `unmeasured_face_refusals`. `InteriorVoidRefusal` and `SectionRecessRefusal` appear in their corresponding families. Record these outcomes in recognition testing as evidence or abstention. Stock surfaces, part properties, support patches, probes and face-accounting bookkeeping have no extra public family rows. [Publication and appended refusals](https://github.com/pzfreo/quiddity/blob/9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051/src/recognition.rs#L374-L451).

Quiddity’s README reports MFInstSeg benchmark results that vary by family. This inventory counts published record labels and does not measure recall or precision. An app feature may provide a ground-truth fixture even when the recognizer fails to recover that label. [Quiddity benchmark summary](https://github.com/pzfreo/quiddity/blob/9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051/README.md#L26-L52), [recognition trust contract](https://github.com/pzfreo/quiddity/blob/9a9709835d385528be5e49369d381bf3589bf051/docs/recognition-trust.md).

**User value** estimates how often a job occurs, how much setup and rework it saves, and downstream leverage in drawings or fabrication. The value order assumes a general-purpose mechanical CAD user base. **Coverage** ranks distinct partly covered or missing published families primarily addressed, with ties broken by missing families first and then by user value. A count is the scope of the proposed slices, including explicit later portions. It is neither an installed detector count nor an estimate of recognition recall. <a id="proposal-a"></a>
### A. Hole stacks and callouts

**Job.** Seat a fastener or fixture pin in a stepped bore and keep its machining dimensions editable together.

**CAD evidence.** SOLIDWORKS Advanced Hole selects a near face, adds ordered elements from either side, and places the stack on its Positions tab. Its type editor covers straight, counterbored, countersunk and tapered-tap elements with standard or custom depths. The 2026 help documents a hole table/callout limitation for advanced holes. Onshape Hole takes sketch points or mate connectors, with separate hole style, drill/clearance/tap selection, depth termination and tip. One Onshape user wanted to choose the countersink end and found the position plane needed to clear the full head, beyond simply locating the centre. [SOLIDWORKS Advanced Hole](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_advanced_holes_divider.htm?id=23.6.25.5) · [Near and far-side elements](https://help.solidworks.com/2025/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/t_creating_advanced_hole_elements.htm) · [Stack parameters](https://help.solidworks.com/2023/English/SolidWorks/sldworks/r_advanced_hole_type_pm.htm?id=4b86273ff0ef4bdb8a98e762a99530b3) · [Onshape Hole](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/PartStudio/hole.htm) · [Onshape user report](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/12370/countersink-hole-from-opposite-face).

**Proposed design.** Extend Hole with ordered, optional near-side and far-side stages. Ship pilot, cylindrical step, spotface, cone and drill tip first. Keep existing defaults and centre picks. Show a labelled section with diameter, included angle, start face, sequential or baseline depth, and a named shoulder/tip convention. Resolve the far end for each centre and persist each stage’s face roles. Derive the drawing callout from the authored stack.

**First validation.** Edit a two-stage counterbore plus spotface, and a stack with a far-side countersink. Verify the core diameter, end identity and modified face roles after reopen and STEP export. Confirm similar shallow counterbores without declared intent stay geometrically ambiguous.

**Roadmap and recognition.** Milestone [1.4](../../../ROADMAP.md#everyday-edge-and-wall) was delivered, and [3.0B](../../../ROADMAP.md#one-model-and-predictor) covers continuation. The new stage editor is new product scope. Closes the compound-hole slice of holes. Also supplies better fixtures for countersinks.

### B. Grid and adaptive feature patterns

**Job.** Lay out mounting holes, vents or repeated recesses without rebuilding a placement sketch after each spacing change.

**CAD evidence.** Onshape Linear Pattern takes a seed, two directions, spacing and count, plus skipped instance handles. Circular Pattern distinguishes angular span from angular step. Reapply Features recomputes instance geometry against its local stock. An Onshape user reported that changing the distance to an edge from 2.51 to 2.55 got one pattern working while two failed, and staff pointed them to Reapply Features. [Onshape Linear Pattern](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/PartStudio/linear_pattern.htm) · [Onshape Circular Pattern](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/PartStudio/circular_pattern.htm) · [Onshape user report](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/27084/linear-feature-pattern-not-generating) · [Slot pattern user report](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/26235/how-to-linear-pattern-a-slot-across-a-face).

**Proposed design.** Add a second direction to the existing Linear Pattern with per-axis count, spacing/span, reversal and skipped grid cells. Show stable row/column indices and preview catch points. Preserve existing one-direction presets. Start with copies of one supported feature and use a later replay mode for local up-to-face geometry. Evaluate face-group seeds separately using explicit target scope and clear provenance.

**First validation.** Save a 3×4 hole grid, omit the corner, change one spacing, and check all surviving instance references. Add complete grids for slot, pocket and section-recess fixtures, a circular cut on a hub, and a variable-stock fixture for the later replay mode.

**Roadmap and recognition.** [C5](../../../ROADMAP.md#drawings-pmi-acceptance) covers recognition of the bracket hole arrangement. The roadmap has no two-direction solid pattern or face-group authoring milestone. Closes grid gaps in hole_patterns, pocket_patterns, slot_patterns and section_recess_patterns. Full circular_face_patterns coverage also needs face-group seeding and adaptive cases. Supports F without counting F’s family twice.

### C. Slot, pocket and profile cuts

**Job.** Place an adjustment slot, cable channel, or locating pocket and control its floor and stop from one editor.

**CAD evidence.** Onshape Extrude Remove uses a selected region and supports depth, up-to limits, offset, draft, and a second direction. A user using Onshape’s sketch Slot expected an exported cut, then learned that the slot needed a separate Extrude Remove. Fusion Sweep Cut can move a profile or solid tool along a path with orientation control. A SOLIDWORKS user asked how to keep a routed cut normal to a curved surface, and users described a swept profile or tool body as workarounds. [Onshape Extrude](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/PartStudio/extrude.htm?cshid=extrude) · [Slot sketch user report](https://www.reddit.com/r/Onshape/comments/19eoec6) · [Fusion Sweep](https://help.autodesk.com/view/fusion360/ENU/?contextId=MODEL-SWEEP-CMD) · [SOLIDWORKS Swept Cut](https://help.solidworks.com/2025/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/r_swept_cut_property_manager.htm) · [Swept-cut user discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/SolidWorks/comments/1q78jif/how_to_achieve_normalto_cut_along_curve/).

**Proposed design.** Wire the roadmap’s Solid Slot to existing Cut using stable references, two centres or a centreline, and width as the default. Add a straight-section preset using rectangle, two floor corner radii, or a single semicircular floor. Ask for run start/end and blind, through, or up-to face. Keep non-slot profiles in Cut and let it reuse existing end conditions. Later add Remove to Sweep for curve-driven cutters with explicit target, path and orientation. Use existing edge treatment for isolated floor rounds where applicable.

**First validation.** Use a mounting slot, an edge-open rectangular recess, separate rounded-U and half-round blind slots, and a recess in cylindrical stock. Check depth/stop edits, face-role stability and recognizer output on exported STEP. Validate app-side exact/tolerance admission for later curved cases.

**Roadmap and recognition.** [Phase 2 App](../../../ROADMAP.md#phase-2-app) names Slot. [2.4](../../../ROADMAP.md#general-sweeps) and Coverage Features [#7](../../../ROADMAP.md#coverage-general-sweeps) target the sweep/termination substrate. Coverage [#5](../../../ROADMAP.md#coverage-spline-booleans-and-fillets)/[#8](../../../ROADMAP.md#coverage-edge-and-wall) address spline stock and floor rounds. Primary gaps are prismatic_pockets, round_bottom_blind_slots, semicircular_blind_slots and section_recesses. The base slots, through-notches, flat pockets, V-channels and step cuts already provide fixture candidates.

### D. Revolved cuts and groove presets

**Proposed design.** Add Remove to Revolve through the existing tool-body subtraction path. **First validation.** Build and edit an OD circlip groove, a bore ring groove, a flat face gland and a conical-rim face groove. Verify common axes, widths and surviving wall/floor identifiers after export.

**Roadmap and recognition.** No dedicated turning or groove product milestone. Coverage Edge and wall [#8](../../../ROADMAP.md#coverage-edge-and-wall) helps treated rims. Closes face_grooves variants and improves authoring for already representable grooves and annular_grooves.

## Catallax recognition and fixture plan

1. **Author intent in SolidDraft.** Exercise named creation handles, save, reopen and edit. Capture size and feature roles from the recipe, including body identity, member indices, support references, and stable face names. Keep tolerances, fits, thread designation and process purpose as explicit data. The modelled geometry supplies the geometric answer.
2. **Export checked geometric cases with independent expectations.** For each feature use an authored case with measured expected dimensions, a rotated or translated equivalent, and a near-neighbour that should abstain or receive a different family. Include split seams, multiple solids, intervening cuts and ambiguous face ownership when applicable. Test nominal/actual depth and interrupted exits explicitly. Use named faces returned by Catallax and a mapping from each export/import rather than comparing Quiddity face indices as persistent identities.
3. **Run native recognition on an import without construction history.** Use Catallax surface kinds, concavity/convexity, axes, connected rim/wall/floor groups and part tolerance. Start with the C5 bracket. Extend first to coaxial stacks, blind recess floors, patterned regions and controlled edge treatments. Keep sibling families and aggregate evidence linked to their source faces. For turned patterns, ambiguous spotfaces and repeated shapes, emit the geometry and its evidence, then consume authored PMI when present.
4. **Cross-check externally.** Run the pinned Quiddity version against the same exported STEP and retain family/parameters/evidence differences. Pair it with independent inspectable expectations. Quiddity’s family definitions and abstentions are reference evidence. Catallax owns the implementation and recognized face bindings. Never infer thread designation, mating purpose, material or fit from geometric similarity alone.
5. **Measure expansion separately from fixture readiness.** Report per-family cases authored, exported and checked, recognition recall on fixtures, erroneous calls and explicit refusals. Stress the part with a later edit before claiming the authoring capability is complete. Track recognition disagreements and kernel refusals with their reproducer, including the native/approximate distinction and the relevant tolerance.
