01Where this starts

  
Picking up where Fornjot left off.

  

    

      
Hanno Braun spent six years on Fornjot, an early-stage B-rep CAD kernel in Rust. In June 2026 he shut it down and wrote up what went wrong. We read that post as a to-do list.

      
Catallax carries on the same goal, a free B-rep kernel in Rust that a CAD application can stand on, and throws AI horsepower and human curiosity at it. AI coding agents write most of the code. A human directs, questions and checks it.

      
Catallax shares no code with Fornjot. It is not affiliated with Hanno Braun and he has not endorsed it. What we take is his published lessons, with thanks.

      Read "Shutting Down Fornjot"
    

    

      
His lessons · what we do about them

      
    
        
  • Early progress does not extrapolateMeasure the hard cases, tangent, coincident and equal radii, and publish the numbers.
    • 
        
  • Do not sell a dreamA works-today list beside every sponsorship ask, including the one further down this page.
    • 
        
  • Incremental change cannot fix an architecturePrototype unknowns with a decision rule. Rebuild only what proves itself.
    • 
        
  • Half-measures sink a projectIn design: SolidDraft runs only on Catallax. Remove its current kernel, with no fallback.
    • 
        
  • A generic kernel blurs the visionIn design: build toward SolidDraft's intended features. Target parts rebuilding at click speed are the scoreboard, not the finish line.
    • 
        
  • Do not leave a redesign half-landedOne representation-wide redesign at a time. Commit or park at each checkpoint.
    • 
      

    

  







  
02Built for what comes next Vision · in design

  
Parametric parts, drawings and assemblies. Lattices, field-driven thickness and simulation-led shapes. SolidDraft grows toward both. The B-rep carries exact faces; the field carries implicit features and checks the B-rep. One definition is the truth.

  
In design: one modelling tolerance chosen before building, separate from display and export accuracy. Approximate only a proven near-contact or a shape the B-rep cannot carry exactly. Record each decision once, merge what is declared the same, carry visible local tolerances and check topology before publishing. Later operations measure against the definition, so tolerances do not accumulate.

  
In design: the field measures approximated pieces against their own definitions. Where it cannot build a step yet, it takes over that B-rep result and checks later steps independently, reporting the coverage. Field-only features need not become B-rep. Read the build order.

SolidDraft. A place to make things.

What works today

      
SolidDraft currently runs on SMLib, not Catallax. This is what the existing application does, not a claim about Catallax integration.

      
    
        
  • Plane and face sketches with lines, rectangles, circles, arcs, slots, relations and dimensions.
    • 
        
  • Extrude, cut, fillet and chamfer. Feature editing, part files, undo and redo, with reference repair when needed.
    • 
        
  • ISO and ANSI drawings with sections, details, dimensions and GD&T, exported as SVG, PDF and DXF.
    • 
        
  • A Claude-connected assistant and MCP commands. A headless CLI for recipe replay, part rebuilds, hidden-line views and drawings. A browser build.
    • 
      

      
Not yet: STEP, IGES or mesh import/export, surfacing and sheet metal. Assemblies are in progress. Nothing leaves the application today except drawings.

    

    

      
What waits on Catallax In design

      
SolidDraft will run only on Catallax. The first target part triggers removal of SMLib, not a choice of backends.

      
    
        
  • Stage 1: sketches, extrudes, cuts, revolves and booleans through Catallax, plus the headless application verification loop.
    • 
        
  • Drawings move to polygon-mode hidden-line removal at the cutover. STEP and mesh export follow through Catallax.
    • 
        
  • Stage 3: holes, chamfers, patterns, mirror, shell, offset and thicken. Stage 4: fillets and draft.
    • 
        
  • Stage 5: sweeps, lofts and spline profiles. Stage 6: STEP import. Field-driven features follow the per-face distance work.
    • 
      

      
In design: commands waiting for a kernel operation stay visible but disabled, with the stage named. Existing parts report a rebuild error at a waiting feature.

08Release discipline

    
Proof before promise.

    
Every pull request faces format, clippy and the full enabled release test suite. Exact maths keeps us honest. OpenCASCADE checks our STEP.

    
Our rule: the same question gives the same answer on every machine and at every thread count. The deterministic catallax-verify exchange uses work budgets to choose outcomes and reports wall time separately. The legacy CLI still has its 50-second deadline.

    
    
      
  • Formatcargo fmt --all --check
    • 
      
  • Lintclippy · deny warnings
    • 
      
  • Testsrelease · all enabled + docs

Catallax works today · the code on main

      
    
        
  • SolidsBlocks, cylinders, spheres, cones and tori. Exact, by definition.
    • 
        
  • BooleansUnite, subtract and intersect on plane, cylinder and sphere pairs, plus closed-form cone and torus cases.
    • 
        
  • SketchesExact line, arc and circle profiles with holes, signed extrusions, rational-frame revolutions and through-all cuts. Operation-derived face and edge names.
    • 
        
  • MeasureVolume, area and centroid with proven bounds.
    • 
        
  • OutputSTEP AP242 with a proven deviation report. Display meshes with proven chord error.
    • 
        
  • FieldRecipe solids, sketches, extrusions, rational-frame revolutions and booleans. Certified distance bounds and a sampled two-way B-rep check, not a whole-model proof.
    • 
      

      
Not yet: fillets, chamfers, shell, offset, draft, sweep, loft, patterns, mirror, STEP import or SolidDraft integration. General algebraic frames and some valid cone, torus and near-tangent cases still refuse. Some operations take seconds; click-speed rebuilds remain in design. The full list, with caveats.

      
Kept rules: no unsafe code; derived geometry stored by definition; a proven answer is never wrong. Only impossible geometry is an acceptable final refusal. Refusals of possible geometry are unfinished work, not exclusions from the goal.
