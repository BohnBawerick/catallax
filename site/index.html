01Why this is free

  
Geometry should not require a vendor's permission.

  

    

      
Nearly every commercial CAD program runs on one of a handful of closed geometry kernels. Their owners licence them, meter them and can withdraw them. The maths inside is a century old and the algorithms are published. What is closed is the code, and the code is kept closed by law.

      
That law is not property. Property exists because things are scarce. If you copy this kernel, we still have it, and now you have one too. Nobody is poorer. Programs are not scarce, so the "intellectual property" over them has to be manufactured by the state: a grant that forbids you from arranging your own bits in a pattern someone else arranged first. Murray Rothbard wrote, "Patents, therefore, are grants of exclusive monopoly privilege by the State" in Man, Economy, and State, chapter 10, section 7. Stephan Kinsella extended the point to copyright: "Ideas are not naturally scarce" in Against Intellectual Property, Journal of Libertarian Studies 15:2, 2001. We stand with that reading.

      
Geometry is mathematics and belongs to everyone. A public kernel lets a one-person CAD product enter the market without buying permission from a kernel vendor. Copy this code. Change it. Sell a product built on it. Compete with us.

      
Catallax puts AI coding agents to work on exact geometry, directed and checked by a human. Most of the inherited implementation was written that way. Whether this approach works at the scale a useful CAD kernel demands is an open question. We will find out in public.

      
Copyright attaches to code by default. We use the legal instrument available to let you use the code without asking us. MIT grants permission to use, copy, modify, publish and sell it, with the notice kept on copies and no warranty.

02What Catallax is

  
A geometry kernel in safe Rust, built for SolidDraft, the CAD application. Two kernels, one recipe: an exact B-rep and a signed distance field evaluate the same feature tree. The B-rep carries exact faces; the field carries implicit features and checks the B-rep. One definition is the truth.

  
Vision · in design Parametric parts, drawings and assemblies. Lattices, field-driven thickness and simulation-led shapes. SolidDraft grows toward both, and runs only on Catallax, with no other kernel as a fallback.

  
In design: one modelling tolerance chosen before building, separate from display and export accuracy. Approximate only a proven near-contact or a shape the B-rep cannot carry exactly. Record each decision once, merge what is declared the same, carry visible local tolerances and check topology before publishing. Later operations measure against the definition, so tolerances do not accumulate.

  
In design: the field measures approximated pieces against their own definitions. Where it cannot build a step yet, it takes over that B-rep result and checks later steps independently, reporting the coverage. Field-only features need not become B-rep. Read the build order.

02Public · early code

The public catallax-recipe/3 format reads, checks and writes the feature tree. Named B-rep builds and the field evaluator consume it today. In design: the rest of the feature language, from chamfers and shells to fillets, sweeps and lofts.






  

    
SolidDraft. A place to make things.

    
A parametric CAD application in Rust, with a feature tree, sketch tools, a ribbon, a 3D viewport and an AI assistant. Catallax and SolidDraft are one project in two repositories.

    
Public under MIT SolidDraft's repository is public. It is not released for download yet; a release with the features is coming soon. For now it is research grade only.

  

  
  

    

      
What works today

      
SolidDraft currently runs on SMLib, not Catallax. This is what the existing application does, not a claim about Catallax integration.

      
    
        
  • Plane and face sketches with lines, rectangles, circles, arcs, slots, relations and dimensions.
    • 
        
  • Extrude, cut, fillet and chamfer. Feature editing, part files, undo and redo, with reference repair when needed.
    • 
        
  • ISO and ANSI drawings with sections, details, dimensions and GD&T, exported as SVG, PDF and DXF.
    • 
        
  • A Claude-connected assistant and MCP commands. A headless CLI for recipe replay, part rebuilds, hidden-line views and drawings. A browser build.
    • 
      

      
Not yet: STEP, IGES or mesh import/export, surfacing and sheet metal. Assemblies are in progress. Nothing leaves the application today except drawings.

    

    

      
What waits on Catallax In design

      
SolidDraft will run only on Catallax. The first target part triggers removal of SMLib, not a choice of backends.

      
    
        
  • Stage 1: sketches, extrudes, cuts, revolves and booleans through Catallax, plus the headless application verification loop.
    • 
        
  • Drawings move to polygon-mode hidden-line removal at the cutover. STEP and mesh export follow through Catallax.
    • 
        
  • Stage 3: holes, chamfers, patterns, mirror, shell, offset and thicken. Stage 4: fillets and draft.
    • 
        
  • Stage 5: sweeps, lofts and spline profiles. Stage 6: STEP import. Field-driven features follow the per-face distance work.
    • 
      

      
In design: commands waiting for a kernel operation stay visible but disabled, with the stage named. Existing parts report a rebuild error at a waiting feature.

08Release discipline

    
Proof before promise.

    
Every pull request faces format, clippy and the full enabled release test suite. Exact maths keeps us honest. OpenCASCADE checks our STEP.

    
Our rule: the same question gives the same answer on every machine and at every thread count. The deterministic catallax-verify exchange uses work budgets to choose outcomes and reports wall time separately. The legacy CLI still has its 50-second deadline.

Catallax works today · the code on main

      
    
        
  • SolidsBlocks, cylinders, spheres, cones and tori. Exact, by definition.
    • 
        
  • BooleansUnite, subtract and intersect on plane, cylinder and sphere pairs, plus closed-form cone and torus cases.
    • 
        
  • SketchesExact line, arc and circle profiles with holes, signed extrusions, rational-frame revolutions and through-all cuts. Operation-derived face and edge names.
    • 
        
  • ChamfersExact named planar strips and conical rings, with equal or ordered unequal setbacks. Independent field construction; general corner and neighbour continuation remain unfinished.
    • 
        
  • MeasureVolume, area and centroid with proven bounds.
    • 
        
  • OutputSTEP AP242 with a proven deviation report. Display meshes with proven chord error.
    • 
        
  • FieldRecipe solids, sketches, extrusions, rational-frame revolutions and booleans. Certified distance bounds and a sampled two-way B-rep check, not a whole-model proof.
    • 
      

      
Not yet: general fillets, chamfer continuation, shell, offset, draft, sweep, loft, patterns, mirror, STEP import or SolidDraft integration. General algebraic frames and some valid cone, torus and near-tangent cases still refuse. Some operations take seconds; click-speed rebuilds remain in design. The full list, with caveats.

      
Kept rules: no unsafe code; derived geometry stored by definition; a proven answer is never wrong. Only impossible geometry is an acceptable final refusal. Refusals of possible geometry are unfinished work, not exclusions from the goal.

    

  







  
10Special thanks

  
Knowledge, published free.

  

    

      
This kernel stands on work that people put into the world for anyone to read: papers, retrospectives, source code and standards, published for free, which is how knowledge should move.

      
None of it entered Catallax as code. What we took is what they wrote down: a definition, a proof, a lesson, a mistake named plainly. Thank you.

      
Catallax shares no code with Fornjot. It is not affiliated with Hanno Braun and he has not endorsed it. The same holds for every project and author below: their published ideas and results, not their source, with thanks.

    

    

      
Published work this kernel learned from

      
    
        
  • Hanno Braun · FornjotSix years on Fornjot, a B-rep kernel in Rust, and the retrospective "Shutting Down Fornjot". Its six named mistakes shaped our build order and the works-today rule beside every sponsorship ask.
    • 
        
  • Matt Keeter · Fidget, libfiveFidget and libfive. The field kernel's fixed-order tapes, independent point evaluation and reusable evaluator storage follow their design.
    • 
        
  • OpenCASCADEThe open-source kernel that opens and checks every STEP file Catallax exports. It never runs inside the kernel.
    • 
        
  • monstertruck, Nacre, ogeom-rsmonstertruck, Nacre and ogeom-rs, Rust B-rep kernels whose public code was a reference point when our B-rep pieces were compared against existing implementations.
    • 
        
  • Raghothama and Shapiro"Consistent updates in dual representation systems" (2000). The method behind checking that two kernels model the same solid.
    • 
        
  • Rossignac and Requicha"Constant-radius blending in solid modeling" (1984) and "Offsetting operations in solid modelling" (1986). The rolling-ball fillet as an offset construction.
    • 
        
  • Farouki and Sverrisson"Approximation of rolling-ball blends for free-form parametric surfaces" (1996). How a blend is approximated to a prescribed precision.
    • 
        
  • Jackson · ParasolidParasolid's tolerant-modelling paper (1995). The reasons behind our local tolerance caps and the rule that tolerance growth must be contained.
    • 
        
  • Hedberg et al."Testing the digital thread" (2016). The gap limits exchanged models carry in automotive and defence work.
    • 
        
  • STEP ToolsPublic documentation of the STEP AP242 entities our exporter writes.
